Integration by Parts

Integration by parts is the primary technique for integrating products of algebraic and transcendental functions. The LIATE rule helps choose $u$.

\[ \int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du \]
Integration by Parts Formula

Key insight: Tabular integration by parts is faster for polynomials multiplied by $e^x$, $\sin(x)$, or $\cos(x)$.

Alternating Series

The alternating harmonic series is a classic example of conditional convergence. It converges by the Alternating Series Test but diverges absolutely.

\[ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n} \]
Alternating Harmonic Series

Shell Method

The shell method computes volumes of revolution by integrating the volume of cylindrical shells. It is especially useful when revolving around the y-axis.

\[ V = 2\pi \int_a^b x \cdot h(x)\,dx \]
Shell Method Volume

Warning: For the shell method, the radius is always the horizontal distance from the axis of rotation. Be careful with horizontal axes of rotation.

Worked Solutions

Problem 1: Integration by Parts

Evaluate: $\int x^2 e^x\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Apply integration by parts with $u = x^2$ and $dv = e^x dx$.

$du = 2x\,dx$ and $v = e^x$.

$$= x^2 e^x - 2\int x e^x\,dx$$

Step 2:

Apply integration by parts again with $u = x$ and $dv = e^x dx$.

$du = dx$ and $v = e^x$.

$$\int x e^x\,dx = x e^x - \int e^x\,dx = x e^x - e^x$$

Step 3:

Substitute back:

$$= x^2 e^x - 2(x e^x - e^x) + C$$

Final Answer:

$$= (x^2 - 2x + 2)e^x + C$$

Problem 2: Series Convergence

Task: Test $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$ for convergence

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Identify this as the alternating harmonic series with $a_n = \frac{1}{n}$.

$a_n = \frac{1}{n} > 0$

$a_n$ is decreasing

$\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = 0$

Final Answer:

All conditions of the Alternating Series Test are satisfied. The series converges.

Problem 3: Volume by Shells

Task: Find the volume when the region bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y = 4$ is revolved about the y-axis.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Find the intersection: $x^2 = 4 \implies x = 2$.

The height of a shell at position $x$ is $4 - x^2$.

Step 2:

Set up the shell method integral:

$$V = 2\pi \int_0^2 x(4-x^2)\,dx$$

$$= 2\pi \int_0^2 (4x - x^3)\,dx$$

Step 3:

Evaluate the integral:

$$= 2\pi \left[2x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2$$

Final Answer:

$$= 2\pi(8 - 4) = 8\pi$$
