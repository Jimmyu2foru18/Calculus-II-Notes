Trigonometric Substitution

Trigonometric substitution handles integrals with radicals of the form $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$, $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$, and $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$ by converting them to standard trigonometric integrals.

\[ \sqrt{4-x^2} \implies x = 2\sin(\theta), \quad dx = 2\cos(\theta)\,d\theta \]
Trig Substitution for $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$

Key insight: When the integrand contains $x^3$ and $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$, split off one factor of $x$ to pair with $dx$, then use $\sin^2(\theta) = 1-\cos^2(\theta)$.

Ratio Test

The Ratio Test is particularly effective for series with factorials or exponential growth. Compute the limit of the ratio of consecutive terms.

\[ \rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| \]
Ratio Test

Parametric Curves

For a curve defined parametrically by $x(t)$ and $y(t)$, the slope of the tangent line is found by dividing the derivatives with respect to $t$.

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} \]
Slope of Tangent to Parametric Curve

Warning: A vertical tangent occurs when $dx/dt = 0$ (and $dy/dt \neq 0$). A horizontal tangent occurs when $dy/dt = 0$ (and $dx/dt \neq 0$).

Worked Solutions

Problem 1: Trig Substitution

Evaluate: $\int \frac{x^3}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Let $x = 2\sin(\theta)$, so $dx = 2\cos(\theta)\,d\theta$.

Then $x^3 = 8\sin^3(\theta)$ and $\sqrt{4-x^2} = 2\cos(\theta)$.

Step 2:

Substitute into the integral:

$$= \int \frac{8\sin^3(\theta) \cdot 2\cos(\theta)\,d\theta}{2\cos(\theta)}$$

$$= 8\int \sin^3(\theta)\,d\theta$$

Step 3:

Use $\sin^2(\theta) = 1-\cos^2(\theta)$ and let $u = \cos(\theta)$:

$$= 8\int (1-u^2)(-du) = -8\left(u - \frac{u^3}{3}\right) + C$$

Final Answer:

Back-substitute $\cos(\theta) = \frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2}$:

$$= -4\sqrt{4-x^2} + \frac{(4-x^2)^{3/2}}{3} + C$$

Problem 2: Series Convergence with Ratio Test

Task: Test $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n^2}{3^n}$ for convergence

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Compute the ratio of consecutive terms:

$$\rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{(n+1)^2/3^{n+1}}{n^2/3^n}$$

Step 2:

Simplify:

$$= \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{(n+1)^2}{3n^2} = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{3}\left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)^2$$

Final Answer:

$$= \frac{1}{3}$$ Since $\rho = \frac{1}{3} < 1$, the series converges by the Ratio Test.

Problem 3: Parametric Curves

Task: Find the slope of the tangent line to the curve $x = \cos(t)$, $y = \sin(t)$ at $t = \pi/4$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Compute the derivatives with respect to $t$:

$\frac{dx}{dt} = -\sin(t)$ and $\frac{dy}{dt} = \cos(t)$.

Step 2:

Compute the slope:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{\cos(t)}{-\sin(t)} = -\cot(t)$$

Final Answer:

At $t = \pi/4$:

$$m = -\cot\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = -1$$

The tangent line has slope $-1$.
