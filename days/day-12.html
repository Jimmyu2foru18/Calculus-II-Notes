Series Convergence Tests

Problem 1: Divergence Test

Task: Test the series sum from n=1 to infinity of 1/(n+1) for convergence.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Apply the Divergence Test

Compute lim(n->infinity) a_n = lim(n->infinity) 1/(n+1) = 0

Step 2: Conclusion

Since the limit is 0, the Divergence Test is inconclusive.

This is a harmonic series shifted by 1, which diverges.

Final Answer:

The series diverges (by comparison to the harmonic series).

Problem 2: Integral Test

Task: Test the series sum from n=1 to infinity of 1/(n*ln(n)) for convergence.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Check conditions

f(x) = 1/(x*ln(x)) is positive, continuous, and decreasing for x > 1.

Step 2: Apply the Integral Test

integral from 2 to infinity of 1/(x*ln(x)) dx

Let u = ln(x), du = 1/x dx

= integral from ln(2) to infinity of 1/u du = lim(b->infinity) [ln(u)] from ln(2) to b

= lim(b->infinity) (ln(b) - ln(ln(2))) = infinity

Final Answer:

The series diverges by the Integral Test.

Problem 3: p-Series Test

Task: Determine if sum from n=1 to infinity of 1/n^(3/2) converges.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify as a p-series

This is a p-series with p = 3/2.

Step 2: Apply the p-Series Test

Since p = 3/2 > 1, the series converges.

Final Answer:

The series converges (p = 3/2 > 1).

Problem 4: Limit Comparison Test

Task: Test the series sum from n=1 to infinity of (n+1)/(n^2+5) for convergence.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Choose a comparison series

For large n, (n+1)/(n^2+5) ~ n/n^2 = 1/n. Compare with sum of 1/n.

Step 2: Compute the limit

lim(n->infinity) [((n+1)/(n^2+5)) / (1/n)] = lim(n->infinity) n(n+1)/(n^2+5)

= lim(n->infinity) (n^2+n)/(n^2+5) = 1

Step 3: Conclusion

Since 0 < L = 1 < infinity, both series behave the same way. Since sum of 1/n diverges, the given series also diverges.

Final Answer:

The series diverges by the Limit Comparison Test.
