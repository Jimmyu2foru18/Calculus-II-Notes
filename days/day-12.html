Convergence Tests Overview

Several tests allow us to determine whether an infinite series converges without finding its exact sum. Each test has specific conditions for use.

Summary of Tests

Divergence Test: If $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n \neq 0$, the series $\sum a_n$ diverges. If the limit is $0$, the test is inconclusive.

Integral Test: If $f(x)$ is positive, continuous, and decreasing for $x \geq 1$, then $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(n)$ and $\int_1^{\infty} f(x)\,dx$ have the same convergence behavior.

p-Series Test: The series $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^p}$ converges if $p > 1$ and diverges if $p \leq 1$.

Limit Comparison Test: If $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = L$ with $0 < L < \infty$, then $\sum a_n$ and $\sum b_n$ have the same behavior.

Problems

Example 1.1

Problem: Test the series $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n + 1}$ for convergence.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Apply the Divergence Test. Compute:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n + 1} = 0$$

Step 2:

The Divergence Test is inconclusive because the limit is $0$. Recognize that this is a harmonic series shifted by $1$: $\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}$, which is known to diverge.

Final Answer:

The series diverges by comparison to the harmonic series.

Example 1.2

Problem: Test the series $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n\ln(n)}$ for convergence.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Apply the Divergence Test: $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n\ln(n)} = 0$. Inconclusive.

Step 2:

Try the Integral Test. Let $f(x) = \frac{1}{x\ln(x)}$. This function is positive, continuous, and decreasing for $x > 1$.

Step 3:

Evaluate the corresponding improper integral:

$$\int_2^{\infty} \frac{1}{x\ln(x)}\,dx = \lim_{b \to \infty} \int_2^b \frac{1}{x\ln(x)}\,dx$$

Let $u = \ln(x)$, $du = \frac{1}{x}\,dx$:

$$= \lim_{b \to \infty} \int_{\ln(2)}^{\ln(b)} \frac{1}{u}\,du = \lim_{b \to \infty} [\ln|u|]_{\ln(2)}^{\ln(b)} = \lim_{b \to \infty} (\ln(\ln(b)) - \ln(\ln(2))) = \infty$$

Final Answer:

The series diverges by the Integral Test.

Example 1.4

Problem: Test the series $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n + 1}{n^2 + 5}$ for convergence.

Figure — Limit Comparison Test regions

The series $\sum (n+1)/(n^2+5)$ (blue) behaves like $\sum 1/n$ (red dashed) for large $n$, so it diverges.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

For large $n$, $\displaystyle \frac{n + 1}{n^2 + 5} \sim \frac{n}{n^2} = \frac{1}{n}$. Compare with $\sum \frac{1}{n}$, which diverges.

Step 2:

Apply the Limit Comparison Test:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\frac{n + 1}{n^2 + 5}}{\frac{1}{n}} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n(n + 1)}{n^2 + 5} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^2 + n}{n^2 + 5} = 1$$

Step 3:

Since $0 < L = 1 < \infty$, both series have the same behavior. Since $\sum \frac{1}{n}$ diverges, the given series also diverges.

Final Answer:

The series diverges by the Limit Comparison Test.

Problem 5 — Test $\displaystyle \sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n\sqrt{\ln(n)}}$

Choose the best test.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Integral Test with $f(x) = \frac{1}{x\sqrt{\ln(x)}}$. Let $u = \ln(x)$, $du = \frac{1}{x}\,dx$:

$$\int_2^{\infty} \frac{1}{x\sqrt{\ln(x)}}\,dx = \lim_{b \to \infty} \int_{\ln(2)}^{\ln(b)} u^{-1/2}\,du = \lim_{b \to \infty} [2u^{1/2}]_{\ln(2)}^{\ln(b)} = \infty$$

The series diverges by the Integral Test.

Problem 6 — Test $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2^n}{n!}$

Try the Ratio Test.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test:

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2^{n+1}/(n+1)!}{2^n/n!} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2}{n + 1} = 0$$

Since $\rho = 0 < 1$, the series converges absolutely.

Study Tips

1. Always start with the Divergence Test. It is quick and can immediately rule out convergence.

2. For series involving $n^p$, $\ln(n)$, or $n!\n$, the Integral Test or Ratio Test are usually the most effective.

3. When using the Limit Comparison Test, choose $b_n$ to be a simple series whose behavior you know (usually a $p$-series or geometric series).

4. If $f(x)$ is difficult to integrate, the Integral Test may not be practical. Consider the Comparison Test instead.

5. The Ratio Test is particularly powerful for series with factorials or exponentials like $a^n$.
