Advanced Convergence Tests

When basic tests are inconclusive, advanced tests like the Ratio Test, Root Test, and Alternating Series Test help determine convergence or divergence.

Test Formulas

Ratio Test: Let $\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|$. If $\rho < 1$, the series converges absolutely. If $\rho > 1$ (or $\rho = \infty$), the series diverges. If $\rho = 1$, the test is inconclusive.

Root Test: Let $\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sqrt[n]{|a_n|}$. If $\rho < 1$, the series converges absolutely. If $\rho > 1$ (or $\rho = \infty$), the series diverges. If $\rho = 1$, the test is inconclusive.

Alternating Series Test: The series $\sum (-1)^n a_n$ converges if $a_n > 0$, $a_{n+1} \leq a_n$ for all $n$, and $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$.

Example 13.1

Problem: Test the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n!}{n^2}$ for convergence.

Figure — Ratio Test convergence diagram

The terms $|a_n| = n!/n^2$ grow rapidly, giving $\rho = \infty > 1$, so the series diverges.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Apply the Ratio Test

$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|$

$a_n = \frac{n!}{n^2}$

$a_{n+1} = \frac{(n+1)!}{(n+1)^2}$

Step 2: Compute the ratio

$\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n} = \frac{(n+1)!/(n+1)^2}{n!/n^2} = \frac{n^2}{n+1}$

Step 3: Take the limit

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^2}{n+1} = \infty$$

Final Answer:

The series diverges by the Ratio Test ($\rho = \infty > 1$).

Example 13.2

Problem: Test the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n$ for convergence.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Apply the Root Test

$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sqrt[n]{|a_n|}$

$a_n = \left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n$

Step 2: Compute the $n$th root

$$\sqrt[n]{\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^n} = \frac{n}{n+1}$$

Step 3: Take the limit

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{n+1} = 1$$

Final Answer:

The Root Test is inconclusive ($\rho = 1$). Another test is needed.

Example 13.3

Problem: Test the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$ for convergence.

Figure — Alternating harmonic series

The alternating harmonic series terms $a_n = (-1)^{n+1}/n$ decrease in magnitude to 0, so it converges.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Check the conditions

$a_n = \frac{1}{n}$

Condition 1: $a_n > 0$ for all $n$ (TRUE)

Condition 2: $a_n$ is decreasing (TRUE, since $\frac{1}{n+1} < \frac{1}{n}$)

Condition 3: $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} = 0$ (TRUE)

Step 2: Conclusion

All conditions are satisfied, so the series converges by the Alternating Series Test.

Final Answer:

The series converges by the Alternating Series Test.

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Ratio Test with Factorial

Test the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n!}{n^3}$ for convergence.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test:

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(n+1)!/(n+1)^3}{n!/n^3} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^3}{n+1} = \infty$$

The series diverges by the Ratio Test.

Problem 2 — Alternating Series with $n^2$

Test the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ for convergence.

Show Answer

Answer:

$a_n = \frac{1}{n^2} > 0$, $a_{n+1} \leq a_n$, and $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$.

The series converges by the Alternating Series Test (and absolutely by the $p$-series test).

Problem 3 — Root Test with Exponential

Test the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{2n}{3n+1}\right)^n$ for convergence.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Root Test:

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2n}{3n+1} = \frac{2}{3} < 1$$

The series converges absolutely by the Root Test.

Study Tips

1. Start with the simplest test (divergence test, geometric, $p$-series) before trying advanced tests.

2. The Ratio Test is especially useful for series with factorials or exponentials.

3. The Root Test works well for series with $n$th powers.

4. When a test is inconclusive, try a different test or combine tests.
