Power Series and Convergence

A power series centered at $a$ has the form $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n (x-a)^n$. The set of values for which the series converges is called the interval of convergence, and half its length is the radius of convergence $R$.

Key Formulas

Power Series:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n (x-a)^n$$

Ratio Test for Power Series:

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_n}\right| |x-a|$$

Radius of Convergence:

$$R = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}\right|$$

Interval of Convergence: $(a-R, a+R)$, possibly including one or both endpoints.

Example 15.1

Problem: Find the interval and radius of convergence for $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}$.

Figure — Power series convergence interval

The series $\sum x^n/n!$ converges for all real $x$, so the interval is $(-\infty, \infty)$ and $R = \infty$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Apply the Ratio Test

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{x^{n+1}/(n+1)!}{x^n/n!}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{|x|}{n+1}$$

Step 2: Determine convergence

For any $x$, $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{|x|}{n+1} = 0 < 1$.

So the series converges for all $x$.

Final Answer:

Interval of Convergence: $(-\infty, \infty)$

Radius of Convergence: $R = \infty$

Example 15.2

Problem: Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}$.

Figure — Radius of convergence $R = 1$

The interval of convergence is $[-1, 1)$ with radius $R = 1$. The left endpoint is included, the right is not.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Apply the Ratio Test

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{x^{n+1}/(n+1)}{x^n/n}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} |x| \frac{n}{n+1} = |x|$$

Step 2: Find where $\rho < 1$

$|x| < 1$, so $-1 < x < 1$

Step 3: Test endpoints

At $x = 1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}$ (harmonic series) diverges.

At $x = -1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges (alternating harmonic series).

Final Answer:

Interval of Convergence: $[-1, 1)$

Radius of Convergence: $R = 1$

Example 15.3

Problem: Find the interval and radius of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n x^n$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Apply the Ratio Test

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{(n+1)x^{n+1}}{n x^n}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{n+1}{n}\right||x| = |x|$$

Step 2: Find where $\rho < 1$

$|x| < 1$, so $-1 < x < 1$

Step 3: Test endpoints

At $x = 1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n$ diverges (terms do not approach 0).

At $x = -1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n(-1)^n$ diverges (terms do not approach 0).

Final Answer:

Interval of Convergence: $(-1, 1)$

Radius of Convergence: $R = 1$

Example 15.4

Problem: Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x-2)^n}{n}$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Apply the Ratio Test

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{(x-2)^{n+1}/(n+1)}{(x-2)^n/n}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} |x-2| \frac{n}{n+1} = |x-2|$$

Step 2: Find where $\rho < 1$

$|x-2| < 1$, so $1 < x < 3$

Step 3: Test endpoints

At $x = 1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges (alternating harmonic series).

At $x = 3$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}$ diverges (harmonic series).

Final Answer:

Interval of Convergence: $[1, 3)$

Radius of Convergence: $R = 1$

Example 15.5

Problem: Find the interval and radius of convergence for $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(2x)^n}{n^2}$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Apply the Ratio Test

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{(2x)^{n+1}/(n+1)^2}{(2x)^n/n^2}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} 2|x| \frac{n^2}{(n+1)^2} = 2|x|$$

Step 2: Find where $\rho < 1$

$2|x| < 1$, so $|x| < \frac{1}{2}$, i.e., $-\frac{1}{2} < x < \frac{1}{2}$

Step 3: Test endpoints

At $x = \frac{1}{2}$: $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges ($p$-series with $p = 2$).

At $x = -\frac{1}{2}$: $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ converges absolutely.

Final Answer:

Interval of Convergence: $[-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}]$

Radius of Convergence: $R = \frac{1}{2}$

Example 15.6

Problem: Differentiate the power series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n$ term by term and find the interval of convergence of the result.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Differentiate term by term

$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n x^{n-1}$$

Step 2: Apply the Ratio Test to the new series

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{(n+1)x^n}{n x^{n-1}}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{n+1}{n}\right||x| = |x|$$

Step 3: Determine convergence

The series converges when $|x| < 1$.

At $x = 1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n$ diverges.

At $x = -1$: $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n(-1)^{n-1}$ diverges.

Final Answer:

Interval of Convergence: $(-1, 1)$

Radius of Convergence: $R = 1$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Interval of Convergence with Factorial

Find the interval and radius of convergence for $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(x-1)^n}{n!}$.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test:

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{|x-1|}{n+1} = 0 < 1$$

The series converges for all $x$. Interval: $(-\infty, \infty)$, $R = \infty$.

Problem 2 — Alternating Power Series

Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^n}{n}$.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test: $\rho = |x|$. Converges for $|x| < 1$.

At $x = 1$: $\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges (alternating harmonic).

At $x = -1$: $\sum \frac{(-1)^{2n}}{n} = \sum \frac{1}{n}$ diverges.

Interval: $(-1, 1]$, $R = 1$.

Problem 3 — Power Series with $n^2$ in Denominator

Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(3x)^n}{n^2}$.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test: $\rho = 3|x|$. Converges when $|x| < \frac{1}{3}$.

At $x = \frac{1}{3}$: $\sum \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges ($p$-series, $p=2$).

At $x = -\frac{1}{3}$: $\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$ converges absolutely.

Interval: $[-\frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{3}]$, $R = \frac{1}{3}$.

Problem 4 — Integrating a Power Series

Integrate the series $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n$ term by term and find the interval of convergence.

Show Answer

Answer:

$$\int \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n \, dx = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}$$

Apply the Ratio Test: $\rho = |x|$. Converges for $|x| < 1$.

At $x = 1$: $\sum \frac{1}{n}$ diverges. At $x = -1$: $\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges.

Interval: $[-1, 1)$, $R = 1$.

Problem 5 — Power Series with Even Powers

Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2n}}{2^n}$.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test:

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{x^{2(n+1)}/2^{n+1}}{x^{2n}/2^n}\right| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{|x|^2}{2} = \frac{x^2}{2}$$

Converges when $\frac{x^2}{2} < 1$, i.e., $|x| < \sqrt{2}$.

At $x = \pm\sqrt{2}$: series becomes $\sum \frac{2^n}{2^n} = \sum 1$, which diverges.

Interval: $(-\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2})$, $R = \sqrt{2}$.

Problem 6 — Centered at $a = -2$

Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x+2)^n}{n \cdot 3^n}$.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test:

$$\rho = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left|\frac{(x+2)^{n+1}/((n+1)3^{n+1})}{(x+2)^n/(n \cdot 3^n)}\right| = \frac{|x+2|}{3}$$

Converges when $\frac{|x+2|}{3} < 1$, i.e., $|x+2| < 3$, so $-5 < x < 1$.

At $x = -5$: $\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$ converges.

At $x = 1$: $\sum \frac{1}{n}$ diverges.

Interval: $[-5, 1)$, $R = 3$.

Study Tips

1. Use the Ratio Test first; it works for most power series.

2. Always check endpoints separately after finding the open interval.

3. If the Ratio Test gives $\rho = |x-a|/R$, then $R$ is the radius of convergence.

4. For power series centered at $a$, the interval is $(a-R, a+R)$.

5. Differentiation and integration preserve the radius of convergence but may change endpoint behavior.
