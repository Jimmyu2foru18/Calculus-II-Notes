Conic Sections and Polar Coordinates

Polar coordinates provide an alternative to rectangular coordinates for describing curves. Conic sections can also be expressed naturally in polar form with a focus at the origin.

Key Formulas

Polar to Rectangular:

$$x = r\cos(\theta), \quad y = r\sin(\theta), \quad x^2 + y^2 = r^2$$

Rectangular to Polar:

$$r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \quad \theta = \arctan\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$$

Area in Polar Coordinates:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} [r(\theta)]^2 \, d\theta$$

Conic Section in Polar Form:

$$r = \frac{ed}{1 + e\cos(\theta)} \quad \text{or} \quad r = \frac{ed}{1 + e\sin(\theta)}$$

where $e$ is the eccentricity and $d$ is the distance from the focus to the directrix.

Example 17.1

Problem: Convert the rectangular equation $x^2 + y^2 = 4x$ to polar form.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Use polar coordinate substitutions

$x = r\cos(\theta)$, $y = r\sin(\theta)$, $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$

Step 2: Substitute

$$r^2 = 4r\cos(\theta)$$

Step 3: Simplify

$$r = 4\cos(\theta) \quad (\text{for } r \neq 0)$$

Final Answer:

The polar form is $r = 4\cos(\theta)$. This is a circle of radius 2 centered at $(2, 0)$.

Example 17.2

Problem: Describe and sketch the graph of $r = 2\cos(3\theta)$.

Figure — 3-petaled rose curve $r = 2\cos(3\theta)$

The rose curve $r = 2\cos(3\theta)$ has 3 petals of length 2.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the type of curve

$r = a\cos(n\theta)$ with $a = 2$ and $n = 3$ is a rose curve.

Step 2: Determine the number of petals

Since $n = 3$ is odd, the rose has $n = 3$ petals.

Step 3: Find key points

$\theta = 0$: $r = 2\cos(0) = 2$

$\theta = \frac{\pi}{6}$: $r = 2\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}) = 0$

$\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$: $r = 2\cos(\pi) = -2$

$\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$: $r = 2\cos(\frac{3\pi}{2}) = 0$

Final Answer:

A 3-petaled rose with petals of length 2, centered at the origin.

Example 17.3

Problem: Find the area enclosed by the curve $r = 2\sin(\theta)$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the curve

$r = 2\sin(\theta)$ is a circle of radius 1 centered at $(0, 1)$.

Step 2: Set up the area integral

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{\pi} (2\sin(\theta))^2 \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{\pi} 4\sin^2(\theta) \, d\theta$$

$$= 2 \int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{2} \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_{0}^{\pi} (1 - \cos(2\theta)) \, d\theta$$

Step 3: Evaluate

$$= \left[\theta - \frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\right]_{0}^{\pi}$$

$$= (\pi - 0) - (0 - 0) = \pi$$

Final Answer:

The area is $\pi$.

Example 17.4

Problem: Convert the polar equation $r = \frac{6}{2 + \cos(\theta)}$ to rectangular form and identify the conic.

Figure — Conic section: ellipse

The polar equation $r = 6/(2+\cos\theta)$ represents an ellipse centered at $(-2, 0)$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Clear the denominator

$$r(2 + \cos(\theta)) = 6$$

$$2r + r\cos(\theta) = 6$$

Step 2: Substitute polar conversions

$$2\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} + x = 6$$

$$2\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = 6 - x$$

Step 3: Square both sides

$$4(x^2 + y^2) = (6-x)^2 = 36 - 12x + x^2$$

$$4x^2 + 4y^2 = 36 - 12x + x^2$$

$$3x^2 + 12x + 4y^2 = 36$$

$$3(x^2 + 4x) + 4y^2 = 36$$

$$3(x+2)^2 + 4y^2 = 48$$

$$\frac{(x+2)^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{12} = 1$$

Final Answer:

Rectangular form: $\frac{(x+2)^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{12} = 1$, which is an ellipse centered at $(-2, 0)$ with semi-major axis 4 and semi-minor axis $2\sqrt{3}$.

Example 17.5

Problem: Describe and sketch the graph of $r = 1 + 2\sin(\theta)$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the type of curve

$r = a + b\sin(\theta)$ with $a = 1$ and $b = 2$ is a limacon.

Since $|b| > |a|$, the limacon has an inner loop.

Step 2: Find key points

$\theta = 0$: $r = 1$

$\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$: $r = 1 + 2 = 3$ (maximum)

$\theta = \pi$: $r = 1$

$\theta = \frac{3\pi}{2}$: $r = 1 - 2 = -1$ (inner loop)

Step 3: Find where $r = 0$

$1 + 2\sin(\theta) = 0 \Rightarrow \sin(\theta) = -\frac{1}{2}$

$\theta = \frac{7\pi}{6}, \frac{11\pi}{6}$

Final Answer:

A limacon with an inner loop, symmetric about the vertical axis, with maximum $r = 3$ and inner loop reaching $r = -1$.

Example 17.6

Problem: Find the area inside the cardioid $r = 1 + \cos(\theta)$.

Figure — Polar area of cardioid $r = 1 + \cos(\theta)$

The cardioid $r = 1 + \cos(\theta)$ encloses an area of $3\pi/2$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Set up the area integral

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} (1 + \cos(\theta))^2 \, d\theta$$

Step 2: Expand the integrand

$$(1 + \cos(\theta))^2 = 1 + 2\cos(\theta) + \cos^2(\theta)$$

$$= 1 + 2\cos(\theta) + \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}$$

$$= \frac{3}{2} + 2\cos(\theta) + \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)$$

Step 3: Integrate

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} \left[\frac{3}{2} + 2\cos(\theta) + \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)\right] d\theta$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \left[\frac{3\theta}{2} + 2\sin(\theta) + \frac{1}{4}\sin(2\theta)\right]_{0}^{2\pi}$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \left[(3\pi) + 0 + 0 - 0\right]$$

$$= \frac{3\pi}{2}$$

Final Answer:

The area is $\frac{3\pi}{2}$.

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Polar to Rectangular Conversion

Convert $r = 2\sin(\theta)$ to rectangular form.

Show Answer

Answer:

$r = 2\sin(\theta) \Rightarrow r^2 = 2r\sin(\theta) \Rightarrow x^2 + y^2 = 2y$.

Completing the square: $x^2 + (y-1)^2 = 1$, a circle of radius 1 centered at $(0, 1)$.

Problem 2 — Area of a Rose Curve

Find the area of one petal of the rose $r = 2\sin(3\theta)$.

Show Answer

Answer:

One petal spans from $\theta = 0$ to $\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$.

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{\pi/3} (2\sin(3\theta))^2 \, d\theta = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{\pi/3} 4\sin^2(3\theta) \, d\theta$$

$$= \int_{0}^{\pi/3} \frac{1 - \cos(6\theta)}{2} \, d\theta = \left[\frac{\theta}{2} - \frac{\sin(6\theta)}{12}\right]_{0}^{\pi/3} = \frac{\pi}{6}$$

Problem 3 — Area Between Polar Curves

Find the area inside the circle $r = 2$ and outside the cardioid $r = 1 + \cos(\theta)$.

Show Answer

Answer:

The curves intersect when $2 = 1 + \cos(\theta) \Rightarrow \cos(\theta) = 1 \Rightarrow \theta = 0$ and by symmetry at $\theta = 2\pi$.

Actually, they intersect at $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = 2\pi$, but the cardioid is inside the circle everywhere except at $\theta = 0$.

Better: The area inside the circle is $\pi(2)^2 = 4\pi$. The area of the cardioid is $\frac{3\pi}{2}$ (from Example 17.6).

Area between curves: $4\pi - \frac{3\pi}{2} = \frac{5\pi}{2}$.

Problem 4 — Conic Section in Polar Form

Identify the conic section given by $r = \frac{4}{1 + 2\sin(\theta)}$.

Show Answer

Answer:

The equation is of the form $r = \frac{ed}{1 + e\sin(\theta)}$ with $e = 2$ and $ed = 4$, so $d = 2$.

Since $e = 2 > 1$, this is a hyperbola.

Study Tips

1. Memorize the polar-to-rectangular conversions; they are essential.

2. For rose curves $r = a\cos(n\theta)$ or $r = a\sin(n\theta)$: if $n$ is odd, there are $n$ petals; if $n$ is even, there are $2n$ petals.

3. To find the area of a polar region, sketch the region first to determine the correct limits of integration.

4. For conic sections in polar form, identify $e$ to determine the type: $e < 1$ (ellipse), $e = 1$ (parabola), $e > 1$ (hyperbola).

5. When converting to rectangular form, multiply by the denominator first to eliminate fractions.
