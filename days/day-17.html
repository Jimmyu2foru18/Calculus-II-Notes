Conic Sections and Polar Coordinates

Problem 1: Polar Coordinates Conversion

Task: Convert the rectangular equation x^2 + y^2 = 4x to polar form.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Use polar coordinate substitutions

x = r*cos(theta), y = r*sin(theta), x^2 + y^2 = r^2

Step 2: Substitute

r^2 = 4r*cos(theta)

Step 3: Simplify

r = 4*cos(theta) (for r != 0)

Final Answer:

The polar form is r = 4*cos(theta). This is a circle of radius 2 centered at (2, 0).

Problem 2: Graphing Polar Curves

Task: Describe and sketch the graph of r = 2*cos(3*theta).

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the type of curve

r = a*cos(n*theta) with a = 2 and n = 3 is a rose curve.

Step 2: Determine the number of petals

Since n = 3 is odd, the rose has n = 3 petals.

Step 3: Find key points

theta = 0: r = 2*cos(0) = 2

theta = pi/6: r = 2*cos(pi/2) = 0

theta = pi/3: r = 2*cos(pi) = -2

theta = pi/2: r = 2*cos(3*pi/2) = 0

Final Answer:

A 3-petaled rose with petals of length 2, centered at the origin.

Problem 3: Area in Polar Coordinates

Task: Find the area enclosed by the curve r = 2*sin(theta).

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the curve

r = 2*sin(theta) is a circle of radius 1 centered at (0, 1).

Step 2: Set up the area integral

A = (1/2) * integral from 0 to pi of (2*sin(theta))^2 d(theta)

= (1/2) * integral from 0 to pi of 4*sin^2(theta) d(theta)

= 2 * integral from 0 to pi of (1 - cos(2*theta))/2 d(theta)

= integral from 0 to pi of (1 - cos(2*theta)) d(theta)

Step 3: Evaluate

= [theta - (1/2)*sin(2*theta)] from 0 to pi

= (pi - 0) - (0 - 0) = pi

Final Answer:

The area is pi.
