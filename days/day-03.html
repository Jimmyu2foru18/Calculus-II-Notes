Integration by Substitution

u-Substitution is the integration counterpart to the chain rule for differentiation. The key formula is:
$$ \int f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx = \int f(u)\,du \quad \text{where } u = g(x), \, du = g'(x)\,dx $$

Problem 1: Basic u-Substitution

Evaluate \( \int \frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\,dx \)

Step 1: Choose \( u = x^2 + 1 \), then \( du = 2x\,dx \)
Step 2: Substitute into the integral:
\begin{aligned} \int \frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\,dx &= \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}\,du \\ &= \int u^{-1/2}\,du \end{aligned}
Step 3: Integrate using the power rule:
\( \int u^{-1/2}\,du = \frac{u^{1/2}}{1/2} + C = 2\sqrt{u} + C \)
Step 4: Back-substitute \( u = x^2 + 1 \):
\( = 2\sqrt{x^2+1} + C \)
$$ \int \frac{2x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\,dx = 2\sqrt{x^2+1} + C $$
Problem 2: Area Between Curves - Intersection Points

Task: Find the area of the region bounded by \( y = x \) and \( y = x^3 \)

Step 1: Find intersection points by solving \( x = x^3 \):
\begin{aligned} x &= x^3 \\ x - x^3 &= 0 \\ x(1-x^2) &= 0 \\ x(1-x)(1+x) &= 0 \end{aligned} So \( x = 0, 1, -1 \)
Step 2: Determine which function is on top on each interval:
On \([-1, 0]\): Test \( x = -0.5 \): \( -0.5 > (-0.5)^3 = -0.125 \), so \( x > x^3 \)
On \([0, 1]\): Test \( x = 0.5 \): \( 0.5 < (0.5)^3 = 0.125 \), wait, \( 0.5 > 0.125 \), so \( x > x^3 \)
Actually, \( x^3 \leq x \) on \([-1, 1]\), so \( x \) is on top.
Step 3: Set up the integral (use symmetry or split at 0):
\begin{aligned} A &= \int_{-1}^1 (x - x^3)\,dx \\ &= 2\int_0^1 (x - x^3)\,dx \quad \text{(by symmetry)} \\ &= 2\left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1 \\ &= 2\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4}\right) \\ &= 2 \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \end{aligned}
$$ A = \frac{1}{2} $$
Problem 3: Area Between Two Parabolas

Task: Find the area bounded by \( y = x^2 \) and \( y = x + 2 \)

Step 1: Find intersection points by solving \( x^2 = x + 2 \):
\begin{aligned} x^2 - x - 2 &= 0 \\ (x+1)(x-2) &= 0 \end{aligned} So \( x = -1, 2 \)
Step 2: On \([-1, 2]\), the line \( y = x+2 \) is above the parabola \( y = x^2 \):
\begin{aligned} A &= \int_{-1}^2 [(x+2) - x^2]\,dx \\ &= \left[\frac{x^2}{2} + 2x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^2 \end{aligned}
Step 3: Evaluate at the bounds:
\begin{aligned} F(2) &= \frac{4}{2} + 4 - \frac{8}{3} = 2 + 4 - \frac{8}{3} = 6 - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{10}{3} \\ F(-1) &= \frac{1}{2} - 2 - \frac{-1}{3} = \frac{1}{2} - 2 + \frac{1}{3} = -\frac{7}{6} \\ A &= \frac{10}{3} - \left(-\frac{7}{6}\right) = \frac{20}{6} + \frac{7}{6} = \frac{27}{6} = \frac{9}{2} \end{aligned}
$$ A = \frac{9}{2} $$
Problem 4: Area of a Circle Segment

Task: Find the area of the region inside the circle \( x^2 + y^2 = 1 \) and above the x-axis, bounded by \( x = -1 \) and \( x = 1 \)

Step 1: The circle has equation \( x^2 + y^2 = 1 \), so \( y = \sqrt{1-x^2} \) (top half)
Step 2: Set up the integral for the upper semicircle:
\begin{aligned} A &= \int_{-1}^1 \sqrt{1-x^2}\,dx \end{aligned}
Step 3: Use the substitution \( x = \sin(\theta) \), \( dx = \cos(\theta)\,d\theta \):
When \( x = -1 \), \( \theta = -\pi/2 \). When \( x = 1 \), \( \theta = \pi/2 \).
\begin{aligned} A &= \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \sqrt{1-\sin^2(\theta)} \cdot \cos(\theta)\,d\theta \\ &= \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \cos^2(\theta)\,d\theta \\ &= \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \frac{1+\cos(2\theta)}{2}\,d\theta \\ &= \frac{1}{2}\left[\theta + \frac{\sin(2\theta)}{2}\right]_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \\ &= \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - \left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\right) = \frac{\pi}{2} \end{aligned}
$$ A = \frac{\pi}{2} $$

Note: The full circle area is \( \pi \), so the upper semicircle has area \( \pi/2 \).

Problem 5: Area with Integration w.r.t. y

Task: Find the area of the region bounded by \( x = y^2 - 4 \) and \( x = 0 \) (the y-axis)

Step 1: Find the y-intercepts of \( x = y^2 - 4 \):
Set \( x = 0 \): \( 0 = y^2 - 4 \implies y^2 = 4 \implies y = \pm 2 \)
So the region extends from \( y = -2 \) to \( y = 2 \)
Step 2: Set up the integral with respect to \( y \):
Right curve: \( x = 0 \), Left curve: \( x = y^2 - 4 \)
\begin{aligned} A &= \int_{-2}^2 [0 - (y^2-4)]\,dy \\ &= \int_{-2}^2 (4-y^2)\,dy \end{aligned}
Step 3: Evaluate (using symmetry):
\begin{aligned} A &= 2\int_0^2 (4-y^2)\,dy \\ &= 2\left[4y - \frac{y^3}{3}\right]_0^2 \\ &= 2\left(8 - \frac{8}{3}\right) \\ &= 2 \cdot \frac{16}{3} = \frac{32}{3} \end{aligned}
$$ A = \frac{32}{3} $$
Problem 6: Volume by Slicing (Disk Method)

Task: Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving \( y = \sqrt{x} \) about the x-axis from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 4 \)

Step 1: The disk method formula is:
\( V = \pi\int_a^b [f(x)]^2\,dx \)
Step 2: Here \( f(x) = \sqrt{x} \), \( a = 0 \), \( b = 4 \):
\begin{aligned} V &= \pi\int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2\,dx \\ &= \pi\int_0^4 x\,dx \\ &= \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4 \\ &= \pi\left(\frac{16}{2} - 0\right) = 8\pi \end{aligned}
$$ V = 8\pi $$
Problem 7: Volume of Solid with Hole (Washer Method)

Task: Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving the region bounded by \( y = 2x \) and \( y = x^2 \) about the x-axis

Step 1: Find intersection points: \( 2x = x^2 \implies x^2 - 2x = 0 \implies x(x-2) = 0 \), so \( x = 0, 2 \)
Step 2: On \([0,2]\), \( 2x \geq x^2 \), so outer radius \( R(x) = 2x \), inner radius \( r(x) = x^2 \)
Step 3: Apply the washer method:
\begin{aligned} V &= \pi\int_0^2 [(2x)^2 - (x^2)^2]\,dx \\ &= \pi\int_0^2 (4x^2 - x^4)\,dx \\ &= \pi\left[\frac{4x^3}{3} - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^2 \\ &= \pi\left(\frac{32}{3} - \frac{32}{5}\right) \\ &= \pi\left(\frac{160 - 96}{15}\right) = \frac{64\pi}{15} \end{aligned}
$$ V = \frac{64\pi}{15} $$
Problem 8: Volume by Slicing about the y-axis

Task: Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving the region bounded by \( y = \sqrt{x} \), \( y = 0 \), \( x = 4 \) about the y-axis

Step 1: Using the disk method with respect to \( y \):
\( x = y^2 \), and the limits are \( y = 0 \) to \( y = 2 \) (since \( y = \sqrt{4} = 2 \))
Step 2: Apply the formula \( V = \pi\int_c^d [g(y)]^2\,dy \):
\begin{aligned} V &= \pi\int_0^2 (y^2)^2\,dy \\ &= \pi\int_0^2 y^4\,dy \\ &= \pi\left[\frac{y^5}{5}\right]_0^2 \\ &= \pi \cdot \frac{32}{5} = \frac{32\pi}{5} \end{aligned}
$$ V = \frac{32\pi}{5} $$
