Problem 1: Basic Derivative Rules Review

Task: Review and apply basic derivative rules.

Derivative Rules Summary

\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dx}[c] = 0 \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1} \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[e^x] = e^x \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[\ln|x|] = \frac{1}{x} \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x)] = \cos(x) \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[\cos(x)] = -\sin(x) \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[\sec(x)] = \sec(x)\tan(x) \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[\csc(x)] = -\csc(x)\cot(x) \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[\tan(x)] = \sec^2(x) \\ &\frac{d}{dx}[\cot(x)] = -\csc^2(x) \end{aligned}

Example 1a: Power and Constant Rules

Find \( f'(x) \) where \( f(x) = 7x^2 + 5x + 7\sqrt{x} \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Rewrite all terms using rational exponents:
\( f(x) = 7x^2 + 5x + 7x^{1/2} \)
Step 2: Apply the power rule \( \frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1} \) to each term:
\begin{aligned} f'(x) &= 7 \cdot 2x^{2-1} + 5 \cdot 1x^{1-1} + 7 \cdot \frac{1}{2}x^{1/2 - 1} \\ &= 14x^1 + 5x^0 + \frac{7}{2}x^{-1/2} \end{aligned}
Step 3: Simplify:
\( f'(x) = 14x + 5 + \frac{7}{2\sqrt{x}} \)
Final Answer:
$$ f'(x) = 14x + 5 + \frac{7}{2\sqrt{x}} $$

Example 1b: Chain Rule

Find \( f'(x) \) where \( f(x) = e^{x^2 + 1} \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Identify outer and inner functions:
Outer: \( e^u \), Inner: \( u = x^2 + 1 \)
Step 2: Apply the chain rule \( \frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \):
\begin{aligned} f'(x) &= e^{x^2+1} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}[x^2 + 1] \\ &= e^{x^2+1} \cdot (2x) \end{aligned}
Final Answer:
$$ f'(x) = 2xe^{x^2+1} $$

Example 1c: Product Rule

Find \( f'(x) \) where \( f(x) = e^x \sin(x^2) \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Identify \( u = e^x \) and \( v = \sin(x^2) \)
Step 2: Compute derivatives:
\( u' = e^x \) and \( v' = \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x \) (chain rule)
Step 3: Apply product rule \( (uv)' = u'v + uv' \):
\begin{aligned} f'(x) &= e^x \cdot \sin(x^2) + e^x \cdot \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x \\ &= e^x\sin(x^2) + 2xe^x\cos(x^2) \end{aligned}
Final Answer:
$$ f'(x) = e^x\sin(x^2) + 2xe^x\cos(x^2) $$
Problem 2: More Derivative Examples

Example 2a

Find \( f'(x) \) where \( f(x) = \sqrt{x^2+1} = (x^2+1)^{1/2} \)

Click to see the solution
\begin{aligned} f'(x) &= \frac{1}{2}(x^2+1)^{-1/2} \cdot 2x \\ &= \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}} \end{aligned}
$$ f'(x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}} $$

Example 2b

Find \( f'(x) \) where \( f(x) = \cos(\cos(x)) \)

Click to see the solution
\begin{aligned} f'(x) &= -\sin(\cos(x)) \cdot (-\sin(x)) \\ &= \sin(\cos(x))\sin(x) \end{aligned}
$$ f'(x) = \sin(\cos(x))\sin(x) $$

Example 2c

Find \( f'(x) \) where \( f(x) = \sin^{-1}(x^2+1) \)

Click to see the solution
Note: \( \frac{d}{dx}[\sin^{-1}(u)] = \frac{u'}{\sqrt{1-u^2}} \)

\begin{aligned} f'(x) &= \frac{2x}{\sqrt{1-(x^2+1)^2}} \end{aligned}
$$ f'(x) = \frac{2x}{\sqrt{1-(x^2+1)^2}} $$

Example 2d

Find \( f'(x) \) where \( f(x) = \cos(x^2)\sin^{-1}(x^3) \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Apply product rule with \( u = \cos(x^2) \), \( v = \sin^{-1}(x^3) \)
Step 2: Compute \( u' = -\sin(x^2) \cdot 2x \) and \( v' = \frac{3x^2}{\sqrt{1-x^6}} \)
\begin{aligned} f'(x) &= -\sin(x^2) \cdot 2x \cdot \sin^{-1}(x^3) + \cos(x^2) \cdot \frac{3x^2}{\sqrt{1-x^6}} \end{aligned}
$$ f'(x) = -2x\sin(x^2)\sin^{-1}(x^3) + \frac{3x^2\cos(x^2)}{\sqrt{1-x^6}} $$
Problem 3: Anti-derivative (Indefinite Integral) Rules

Anti-derivatives reverse the process of differentiation. If \( F'(x) = f(x) \), then \( F(x) \) is an anti-derivative of \( f(x) \), written as:
$$ \int f(x)\,dx = F(x) + C $$

Basic Integration Rules

\begin{aligned} &\int x^n\,dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C, \quad n \neq -1 \\ &\int e^x\,dx = e^x + C \\ &\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \ln|x| + C \\ &\int \sin(x)\,dx = -\cos(x) + C \\ &\int \cos(x)\,dx = \sin(x) + C \\ &\int \sec^2(x)\,dx = \tan(x) + C \\ &\int \csc^2(x)\,dx = -\cot(x) + C \\ &\int \sec(x)\tan(x)\,dx = \sec(x) + C \\ &\int \csc(x)\cot(x)\,dx = -\csc(x) + C \\ &\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx = \sin^{-1}(x) + C \\ &\int \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx = \tan^{-1}(x) + C \\ &\int \frac{1}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}}\,dx = \sec^{-1}(x) + C \end{aligned}

Example 3a

Find \( \int (x^2 - 5 + 7x^{-1} + 8x^{-2})\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Apply linearity of integration:
\( \int (x^2 - 5 + 7x^{-1} + 8x^{-2})\,dx = \int x^2\,dx - \int 5\,dx + \int 7x^{-1}\,dx + \int 8x^{-2}\,dx \)
Step 2: Integrate each term:
\begin{aligned} \int x^2\,dx &= \frac{x^3}{3} \\ \int 5\,dx &= 5x \\ \int 7x^{-1}\,dx &= 7\ln|x| \\ \int 8x^{-2}\,dx &= 8 \cdot \frac{x^{-1}}{-1} = -\frac{8}{x} \end{aligned}
$$ \int (x^2 - 5 + 7x^{-1} + 8x^{-2})\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3} - 5x + 7\ln|x| - \frac{8}{x} + C $$

Example 3b

Find \( \int (3x+1)^2\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Expand \( (3x+1)^2 = 9x^2 + 6x + 1 \)
Step 2: Integrate term by term:
\begin{aligned} \int (9x^2 + 6x + 1)\,dx &= 9 \cdot \frac{x^3}{3} + 6 \cdot \frac{x^2}{2} + x \\ &= 3x^3 + 3x^2 + x \end{aligned}
$$ \int (3x+1)^2\,dx = 3x^3 + 3x^2 + x + C $$

Example 3c

Find \( \int [4(1+x^2)^{-1} + \sec^2(x)]\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
\begin{aligned} \int [4(1+x^2)^{-1} + \sec^2(x)]\,dx &= 4\int \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx + \int \sec^2(x)\,dx \\ &= 4\tan^{-1}(x) + \tan(x) + C \end{aligned}
$$ \int [4(1+x^2)^{-1} + \sec^2(x)]\,dx = 4\tan^{-1}(x) + \tan(x) + C $$

Example 3d

Find \( \int \sqrt{\sin^2(e^x) + \cos^2(e^x)}\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Use the Pythagorean identity \( \sin^2(\theta) + \cos^2(\theta) = 1 \):
\( \sqrt{\sin^2(e^x) + \cos^2(e^x)} = \sqrt{1} = 1 \)
Step 2: Integrate:
\( \int 1\,dx = x + C \)
$$ \int \sqrt{\sin^2(e^x) + \cos^2(e^x)}\,dx = x + C $$

Example 3e

Find \( \int (\sqrt[3]{x} + \sqrt[5]{x^3} - x^{-3/4} - 5)\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Rewrite with rational exponents: \( x^{1/3} + x^{3/5} - x^{-3/4} - 5 \)
Step 2: Integrate term by term:
\begin{aligned} \int x^{1/3}\,dx &= \frac{x^{4/3}}{4/3} = \frac{3}{4}x^{4/3} \\ \int x^{3/5}\,dx &= \frac{x^{8/5}}{8/5} = \frac{5}{8}x^{8/5} \\ \int (-x^{-3/4})\,dx &= -\frac{x^{1/4}}{1/4} = -4x^{1/4} \\ \int (-5)\,dx &= -5x \end{aligned}
$$ \int (\sqrt[3]{x} + \sqrt[5]{x^3} - x^{-3/4} - 5)\,dx = \frac{3}{4}x^{4/3} + \frac{5}{8}x^{8/5} - 4x^{1/4} - 5x + C $$
Problem 4: u-Substitution (Integration by Substitution)

The substitution method uses the formula \( \int f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx = \int f(u)\,du \) where \( u = g(x) \) and \( du = g'(x)\,dx \).

Example 4a: Basic Substitution

Evaluate \( \int e^{7x}\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = 7x \), then \( du = 7\,dx \), so \( dx = \frac{du}{7} \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\( \int e^{7x}\,dx = \int e^u \cdot \frac{du}{7} = \frac{1}{7}\int e^u\,du \)
Step 3: Integrate and back-substitute:
\( = \frac{1}{7}e^u + C = \frac{1}{7}e^{7x} + C \)
$$ \int e^{7x}\,dx = \frac{1}{7}e^{7x} + C $$

Example 4b

Evaluate \( \int (x^3+1)^{3}x^2\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = x^3 + 1 \), then \( du = 3x^2\,dx \), so \( x^2\,dx = \frac{du}{3} \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\( \int u^3 \cdot \frac{du}{3} = \frac{1}{3}\int u^3\,du \)
Step 3: Integrate:
\( = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{u^4}{4} + C = \frac{u^4}{12} + C \)
Step 4: Back-substitute:
\( = \frac{(x^3+1)^4}{12} + C \)
$$ \int (x^3+1)^3 x^2\,dx = \frac{(x^3+1)^4}{12} + C $$

Example 4c

Evaluate \( \int \frac{x^3}{1+x^4}\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = 1 + x^4 \), then \( du = 4x^3\,dx \), so \( x^3\,dx = \frac{du}{4} \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\( \int \frac{1}{u} \cdot \frac{du}{4} = \frac{1}{4}\int \frac{1}{u}\,du \)
Step 3: Integrate:
\( = \frac{1}{4}\ln|u| + C = \frac{1}{4}\ln|1+x^4| + C \)
$$ \int \frac{x^3}{1+x^4}\,dx = \frac{1}{4}\ln(1+x^4) + C $$

Example 4d

Evaluate \( \int \frac{dx}{1+x^2} \) using \( u = x^2 \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = x^2 \), then \( du = 2x\,dx \), so \( dx = \frac{du}{2x} \)
Step 2: This substitution doesn't simplify well. Instead, recognize the standard form:
\( \int \frac{dx}{1+x^2} = \tan^{-1}(x) + C \)
$$ \int \frac{dx}{1+x^2} = \tan^{-1}(x) + C $$

Example 4e

Evaluate \( \int \sin(5x)\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = 5x \), then \( du = 5\,dx \), so \( dx = \frac{du}{5} \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\( \int \sin(u) \cdot \frac{du}{5} = \frac{1}{5}\int \sin(u)\,du \)
Step 3: Integrate:
\( = \frac{1}{5}(-\cos(u)) + C = -\frac{1}{5}\cos(5x) + C \)
$$ \int \sin(5x)\,dx = -\frac{1}{5}\cos(5x) + C $$

Example 4f

Evaluate \( \int \sqrt{x^5+1}\,x^4\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = x^5 + 1 \), then \( du = 5x^4\,dx \), so \( x^4\,dx = \frac{du}{5} \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\( \int u^{1/2} \cdot \frac{du}{5} = \frac{1}{5}\int u^{1/2}\,du \)
Step 3: Integrate:
\( = \frac{1}{5} \cdot \frac{u^{3/2}}{3/2} + C = \frac{2}{15}u^{3/2} + C \)
Step 4: Back-substitute:
\( = \frac{2}{15}(x^5+1)^{3/2} + C \)
$$ \int \sqrt{x^5+1}\,x^4\,dx = \frac{2}{15}(x^5+1)^{3/2} + C $$

Example 4g

Evaluate \( \int \frac{x^4}{\sqrt{x^5+1}}\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = x^5 + 1 \), then \( du = 5x^4\,dx \), so \( x^4\,dx = \frac{du}{5} \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\( \int \frac{x^4}{\sqrt{x^5+1}}\,dx = \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{u}} \cdot \frac{du}{5} = \frac{1}{5}\int u^{-1/2}\,du \)
Step 3: Integrate:
\( = \frac{1}{5} \cdot 2u^{1/2} + C = \frac{2}{5}\sqrt{u} + C \)
Step 4: Back-substitute:
\( = \frac{2}{5}\sqrt{x^5+1} + C \)
$$ \int \frac{x^4}{\sqrt{x^5+1}}\,dx = \frac{2}{5}\sqrt{x^5+1} + C $$
Problem 5: Advanced Substitution Examples

Example 5a

Evaluate \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}(1+\sqrt[4]{x})} \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = \sqrt[4]{x} = x^{1/4} \), then \( x = u^4 \), \( dx = 4u^3\,du \), and \( \sqrt{x} = u^2 \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\begin{aligned} \int \frac{4u^3\,du}{u^2(1+u)} &= 4\int \frac{u}{1+u}\,du \\ &= 4\int \left(1 - \frac{1}{1+u}\right)\,du \\ &= 4\left[u - \ln|1+u|\right] + C \end{aligned}
$$ \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}(1+\sqrt[4]{x})} = 4\sqrt[4]{x} - 4\ln(1+\sqrt[4]{x}) + C $$

Example 5b

Evaluate \( \int \frac{e^x}{1+e^x}\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = 1 + e^x \), then \( du = e^x\,dx \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\( \int \frac{1}{u}\,du = \ln|u| + C = \ln(1+e^x) + C \)
$$ \int \frac{e^x}{1+e^x}\,dx = \ln(1+e^x) + C $$

Example 5c

Evaluate \( \int \frac{e^x}{1+e^{2x}}\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = e^x \), then \( du = e^x\,dx \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\( \int \frac{du}{1+u^2} = \tan^{-1}(u) + C = \tan^{-1}(e^x) + C \)
$$ \int \frac{e^x}{1+e^{2x}}\,dx = \tan^{-1}(e^x) + C $$

Example 5d

Evaluate \( \int \sqrt{x+1}\,x\,dx \)

Click to see the solution
Step 1: Let \( u = x+1 \), so \( x = u-1 \), and \( dx = du \)
Step 2: Substitute:
\begin{aligned} \int \sqrt{u}(u-1)\,du &= \int (u^{3/2} - u^{1/2})\,du \\ &= \frac{u^{5/2}}{5/2} - \frac{u^{3/2}}{3/2} + C \\ &= \frac{2}{5}u^{5/2} - \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2} + C \end{aligned}
$$ \int \sqrt{x+1}\,x\,dx = \frac{2}{5}(x+1)^{5/2} - \frac{2}{3}(x+1)^{3/2} + C $$
Problem 6: Summary of Key Concepts

This section provides a comprehensive review of the fundamental concepts introduced in Day 1.

Relationship Between Derivative and Integral

Derivative (Calculus I): \( f(x) = x^2 \implies f'(x) = 2x \) (Rate of change, slope of tangent)
Anti-derivative (Calculus II): \( \int 2x\,dx = x^2 + C \) (Reverse process, accumulation)
