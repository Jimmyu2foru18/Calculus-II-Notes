Integration by Parts

The integration by parts formula comes from the product rule for differentiation:

$$\frac{d}{dx}[u \cdot v] = u'v + uv'$$

Integrating both sides:

$$\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du$$

LIATE Method for Choosing u

When applying integration by parts, choose $u$ from the following order (L-I-A-T-E):

  1. L - Logarithmic functions: $\ln(x), \log(x)$
  2. I - Inverse trig functions: $\sin^{-1}(x), \tan^{-1}(x)$
  3. A - Algebraic functions: $x, x^2, x^3$
  4. T - Trigonometric functions: $\sin(x), \cos(x)$
  5. E - Exponential functions: $e^x, e^{2x}$

Example 1.1

Problem: Evaluate $\int x e^x\,dx$

Figure — Area interpretation of integration by parts

Integration by parts relates the area under $xe^x$ to the area under $e^x$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Choose u and dv

Using LIATE: Algebraic ($x$) comes before Exponential ($e^x$), so:

$$u = x, \quad dv = e^x\,dx$$

Step 2: Compute du and v

$$du = dx, \quad v = \int e^x\,dx = e^x$$

Step 3: Apply the integration by parts formula

$$\int x e^x\,dx = x \cdot e^x - \int e^x\,dx$$ $$\int x e^x\,dx = x e^x - e^x + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int x e^x\,dx = x e^x - e^x + C}$$

Example 1.2

Problem: Evaluate $\int x^2 \ln(x)\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Choose u and dv

Using LIATE: Logarithmic ($\ln(x)$) comes before Algebraic ($x^2$):

$$u = \ln(x), \quad dv = x^2\,dx$$

Step 2: Compute du and v

$$du = \frac{1}{x}\,dx, \quad v = \int x^2\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3}$$

Step 3: Apply integration by parts

$$\int x^2 \ln(x)\,dx = \ln(x) \cdot \frac{x^3}{3} - \int \frac{x^3}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\,dx$$ $$= \frac{x^3}{3}\ln(x) - \frac{1}{3}\int x^2\,dx$$ $$= \frac{x^3}{3}\ln(x) - \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{x^3}{3} + C$$ $$= \frac{x^3}{3}\ln(x) - \frac{x^3}{9} + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int x^2 \ln(x)\,dx = \frac{x^3}{3}\ln(x) - \frac{x^3}{9} + C}$$

Example 1.3

Problem: Evaluate $\int x^2 \cos(2x)\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1: First application of integration by parts

Choose $u = x^2$ and $dv = \cos(2x)\,dx$:

$$du = 2x\,dx, \quad v = \int \cos(2x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x)$$ $$\int x^2 \cos(2x)\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}\sin(2x) - \int \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x) \cdot 2x\,dx$$ $$= \frac{x^2}{2}\sin(2x) - \int x\sin(2x)\,dx$$

Step 2: Second application of integration by parts

For $\int x\sin(2x)\,dx$, choose $u = x$ and $dv = \sin(2x)\,dx$:

$$du = dx, \quad v = \int \sin(2x)\,dx = -\frac{1}{2}\cos(2x)$$ $$\int x\sin(2x)\,dx = -\frac{x}{2}\cos(2x) - \int \left(-\frac{1}{2}\cos(2x)\right)\,dx$$ $$= -\frac{x}{2}\cos(2x) + \frac{1}{2}\int \cos(2x)\,dx$$ $$= -\frac{x}{2}\cos(2x) + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x)$$ $$= -\frac{x}{2}\cos(2x) + \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x)$$

Step 3: Combine results

$$\int x^2 \cos(2x)\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}\sin(2x) - \left(-\frac{x}{2}\cos(2x) + \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x)\right) + C$$ $$= \frac{x^2}{2}\sin(2x) + \frac{x}{2}\cos(2x) - \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x) + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int x^2 \cos(2x)\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}\sin(2x) + \frac{x}{2}\cos(2x) - \frac{1}{4}\sin(2x) + C}$$

Example 1.4

Problem: Evaluate $\int x^3 e^{2x}\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1: First integration by parts

Choose $u = x^3$ and $dv = e^{2x}\,dx$:

$$du = 3x^2\,dx, \quad v = \frac{1}{2}e^{2x}$$ $$\int x^3 e^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x^3}{2}e^{2x} - \frac{3}{2}\int x^2 e^{2x}\,dx$$

Step 2: Second integration by parts on $\int x^2 e^{2x}\,dx$

Choose $u = x^2$ and $dv = e^{2x}\,dx$:

$$du = 2x\,dx, \quad v = \frac{1}{2}e^{2x}$$ $$\int x^2 e^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}e^{2x} - \int x e^{2x}\,dx$$

Step 3: Third integration by parts on $\int x e^{2x}\,dx$

Choose $u = x$ and $dv = e^{2x}\,dx$:

$$du = dx, \quad v = \frac{1}{2}e^{2x}$$ $$\int x e^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x}{2}e^{2x} - \frac{1}{2}\int e^{2x}\,dx$$ $$= \frac{x}{2}e^{2x} - \frac{1}{4}e^{2x}$$

Step 4: Back-substitute

$$\int x^2 e^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}e^{2x} - \left(\frac{x}{2}e^{2x} - \frac{1}{4}e^{2x}\right)$$ $$= \frac{x^2}{2}e^{2x} - \frac{x}{2}e^{2x} + \frac{1}{4}e^{2x}$$

Step 5: Final result

$$\int x^3 e^{2x}\,dx = \frac{x^3}{2}e^{2x} - \frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{x^2}{2}e^{2x} - \frac{x}{2}e^{2x} + \frac{1}{4}e^{2x}\right)$$ $$= \frac{x^3}{2}e^{2x} - \frac{3x^2}{4}e^{2x} + \frac{3x}{4}e^{2x} - \frac{3}{8}e^{2x} + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int x^3 e^{2x}\,dx = \frac{e^{2x}}{8}(4x^3 - 6x^2 + 6x - 3) + C}$$

Trigonometric Integrals

Key identities for trigonometric integrals:

  • $\sin^2(x) = \frac{1 - \cos(2x)}{2}$
  • $\cos^2(x) = \frac{1 + \cos(2x)}{2}$
  • $\sin^2(x) = 1 - \cos^2(x)$
  • $\cos^2(x) = 1 - \sin^2(x)$

Example 2.1

Problem: Evaluate $\int \sin^3(x)\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Use the identity $\sin^2(x) = 1 - \cos^2(x)$

$$\int \sin^3(x)\,dx = \int \sin^2(x) \cdot \sin(x)\,dx$$ $$= \int (1 - \cos^2(x))\sin(x)\,dx$$

Step 2: Use substitution

Let $u = \cos(x)$, then $du = -\sin(x)\,dx$, so $\sin(x)\,dx = -du$:

$$= \int (1 - u^2)(-du)$$ $$= \int (u^2 - 1)\,du$$

Step 3: Integrate

$$= \frac{u^3}{3} - u + C$$

Step 4: Back-substitute

$$= \frac{\cos^3(x)}{3} - \cos(x) + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int \sin^3(x)\,dx = \frac{\cos^3(x)}{3} - \cos(x) + C}$$

Example 2.2

Problem: Evaluate $\int \cos^4(x)\sin(x)\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the substitution

Since we have $\sin(x)\,dx$ (which is the derivative of $\cos(x)$), let:

$$u = \cos(x), \quad du = -\sin(x)\,dx$$

So $\sin(x)\,dx = -du$:

$$\int \cos^4(x)\sin(x)\,dx = \int u^4 (-du) = -\int u^4\,du$$

Step 2: Integrate

$$= -\frac{u^5}{5} + C$$

Step 3: Back-substitute

$$= -\frac{\cos^5(x)}{5} + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int \cos^4(x)\sin(x)\,dx = -\frac{\cos^5(x)}{5} + C}$$

Example 2.3

Problem: Evaluate $\int \cos^5(x)\sin^3(x)\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Use the identity $\sin^2(x) = 1 - \cos^2(x)$

$$\int \cos^5(x)\sin^3(x)\,dx = \int \cos^5(x)\sin^2(x)\sin(x)\,dx$$ $$= \int \cos^5(x)(1 - \cos^2(x))\sin(x)\,dx$$

Step 2: Use substitution

Let $u = \cos(x)$, then $du = -\sin(x)\,dx$:

$$= \int u^5(1 - u^2)(-du)$$ $$= \int (u^7 - u^5)\,du$$

Step 3: Integrate

$$= \frac{u^8}{8} - \frac{u^6}{6} + C$$

Step 4: Back-substitute

$$= \frac{\cos^8(x)}{8} - \frac{\cos^6(x)}{6} + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int \cos^5(x)\sin^3(x)\,dx = \frac{\cos^8(x)}{8} - \frac{\cos^6(x)}{6} + C}$$

Example 2.4

Problem: Evaluate $\int \sin^2(3x)\,dx$

Figure — Graph of $y = \sin^2(3x)$

The function $y = \sin^2(3x)$ has period $\pi/3$ and oscillates between 0 and 1.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Use the half-angle identity

$$\sin^2(\theta) = \frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{2}$$

With $\theta = 3x$:

$$\sin^2(3x) = \frac{1 - \cos(6x)}{2}$$

Step 2: Substitute and integrate

$$\int \sin^2(3x)\,dx = \int \frac{1 - \cos(6x)}{2}\,dx$$ $$= \frac{1}{2}\int (1 - \cos(6x))\,dx$$ $$= \frac{1}{2}\left[x - \frac{1}{6}\sin(6x)\right] + C$$ $$= \frac{x}{2} - \frac{\sin(6x)}{12} + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int \sin^2(3x)\,dx = \frac{x}{2} - \frac{\sin(6x)}{12} + C}$$

Example 2.5

Problem: Evaluate $\int \sin^4(2x)\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Use the half-angle identity twice

$$\sin^2(\theta) = \frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{2}$$

With $\theta = 2x$:

$$\sin^2(2x) = \frac{1 - \cos(4x)}{2}$$

Therefore:

$$\sin^4(2x) = \left(\frac{1 - \cos(4x)}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1 - 2\cos(4x) + \cos^2(4x)}{4}$$

Step 2: Apply the identity again to $\cos^2(4x)$

$$\cos^2(4x) = \frac{1 + \cos(8x)}{2}$$

So:

$$\sin^4(2x) = \frac{1}{4}\left[1 - 2\cos(4x) + \frac{1 + \cos(8x)}{2}\right]$$ $$= \frac{1}{4}\left[\frac{3}{2} - 2\cos(4x) + \frac{\cos(8x)}{2}\right]$$ $$= \frac{3}{8} - \frac{\cos(4x)}{2} + \frac{\cos(8x)}{8}$$

Step 3: Integrate term by term

$$\int \sin^4(2x)\,dx = \int \left(\frac{3}{8} - \frac{\cos(4x)}{2} + \frac{\cos(8x)}{8}\right)\,dx$$ $$= \frac{3x}{8} - \frac{\sin(4x)}{8} + \frac{\sin(8x)}{64} + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int \sin^4(2x)\,dx = \frac{3x}{8} - \frac{\sin(4x)}{8} + \frac{\sin(8x)}{64} + C}$$

Trigonometric Substitution

For integrals involving $\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$, $\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}$, or $\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}$:

  • $\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$: Use $x = a\sin(\theta)$
  • $\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}$: Use $x = a\tan(\theta)$
  • $\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}$: Use $x = a\sec(\theta)$

Example 3.1

Problem: Evaluate $\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{9-x^2}}$

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the substitution

We have $\sqrt{9-x^2} = \sqrt{a^2-x^2}$ with $a = 3$. Use $x = 3\sin(\theta)$:

$$x = 3\sin(\theta), \quad dx = 3\cos(\theta)\,d\theta$$

Then:

$$\sqrt{9-x^2} = \sqrt{9 - 9\sin^2(\theta)} = 3\sqrt{1-\sin^2(\theta)} = 3\cos(\theta)$$

Step 2: Substitute

$$\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{9-x^2}} = \int \frac{3\cos(\theta)\,d\theta}{3\cos(\theta)}$$ $$= \int d\theta = \theta + C$$

Step 3: Back-substitute

Since $x = 3\sin(\theta)$, we have $\sin(\theta) = \frac{x}{3}$, so $\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)$:

$$= \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{3}\right) + C$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{9-x^2}} = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{3}\right) + C}$$

Summary of Trigonometric Substitutions

FormSubstitutionIdentity Used
$\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$$x = a\sin(\theta)$$1-\sin^2(\theta) = \cos^2(\theta)$
$\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$$x = a\tan(\theta)$$1+\tan^2(\theta) = \sec^2(\theta)$
$\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$$x = a\sec(\theta)$$\sec^2(\theta)-1 = \tan^2(\theta)$

Study Tips

1. Memorize the LIATE rule for integration by parts. When in doubt, choose $u$ to be the function that becomes simpler when differentiated.

2. For trigonometric integrals, check if the power of sine or cosine is odd. If so, save one factor for the $du$ substitution and convert the rest using $\sin^2(x) + \cos^2(x) = 1$.

3. For even powers of sine and cosine, use the half-angle identities repeatedly to reduce the powers.

4. In trigonometric substitution, always draw a right triangle to help with back-substitution. Label the sides based on your substitution and the original radical.
