Surface Area of Revolution

The surface area of a solid of revolution is found by integrating the lateral surface area of infinitesimal frustums (truncated cones) along the curve.

Surface Area Formulas

  • Revolution about the x-axis: $S = 2\pi \int_a^b y\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2}\,dx$
  • Revolution about the y-axis: $S = 2\pi \int_a^b x\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2}\,dx$
  • Parametric curve: $S = 2\pi \int_\alpha^\beta y(t)\sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2}\,dt$

Example 5.1

Problem: Find the surface area generated by rotating $y = \sqrt{x}$ from $x = 1$ to $x = 4$ about the x-axis.

Figure — Surface of revolution for $y = \sqrt{x}$

Rotating $y = \sqrt{x}$ about the x-axis from $x=1$ to $x=4$ generates a surface of revolution.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Compute the derivative

For $y = \sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2}x^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$$

Step 2: Simplify the integrand

Compute $1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2$:

$$1 + \left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}\right)^2 = 1 + \frac{1}{4x} = \frac{4x+1}{4x}$$

Therefore:

$$\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{4x+1}{4x}} = \frac{\sqrt{4x+1}}{2\sqrt{x}}$$

Step 3: Set up the surface area integral

The surface area about the x-axis is:

$$S = 2\pi \int_1^4 y\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2}\,dx$$

Substitute $y = \sqrt{x}$ and the simplified radical:

$$S = 2\pi \int_1^4 \sqrt{x} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{4x+1}}{2\sqrt{x}}\,dx$$

The $\sqrt{x}$ terms cancel:

$$S = \pi \int_1^4 \sqrt{4x+1}\,dx$$

Step 4: Evaluate using u-substitution

Let $u = 4x+1$, so $du = 4\,dx$ and $dx = \frac{du}{4}$.

When $x = 1$, $u = 5$. When $x = 4$, $u = 17$.

$$S = \pi \int_5^{17} \sqrt{u} \cdot \frac{du}{4} = \frac{\pi}{4} \int_5^{17} u^{1/2}\,du$$

Step 5: Integrate and evaluate

$$S = \frac{\pi}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \left[u^{3/2}\right]_5^{17}$$ $$S = \frac{\pi}{6} \left(17^{3/2} - 5^{3/2}\right)$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{S = \frac{\pi}{6}\left(17\sqrt{17} - 5\sqrt{5}\right)}$$

Example 5.2

Problem: Find the surface area generated by rotating $y = x^2$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 1$ about the y-axis.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Compute the derivative

For $y = x^2$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x$$

Step 2: Simplify the integrand

Compute $1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2$:

$$1 + (2x)^2 = 1 + 4x^2$$

Therefore:

$$\sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} = \sqrt{1+4x^2}$$

Step 3: Set up the surface area integral about the y-axis

When rotating about the y-axis, the radius of each shell is $x$:

$$S = 2\pi \int_0^1 x\sqrt{1+4x^2}\,dx$$

Step 4: Evaluate using u-substitution

Let $u = 1+4x^2$, so $du = 8x\,dx$ and $x\,dx = \frac{du}{8}$.

When $x = 0$, $u = 1$. When $x = 1$, $u = 5$.

$$S = 2\pi \int_1^5 \sqrt{u} \cdot \frac{du}{8} = \frac{\pi}{4} \int_1^5 u^{1/2}\,du$$

Step 5: Integrate and evaluate

$$S = \frac{\pi}{4} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^5$$ $$S = \frac{\pi}{6} \left(5^{3/2} - 1\right)$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{S = \frac{\pi}{6}\left(5\sqrt{5} - 1\right)}$$

Work

In physics, work is the product of force and displacement. When force varies with position, work is computed by integration.

Work Formulas

  • Constant force: $W = F \cdot d$
  • Variable force: $W = \int_a^b F(x)\,dx$
  • Spring (Hooke's Law): $F = kx$, where $k$ is the spring constant
  • Lifting a rope of linear density $\rho$: $W = \int_0^L \rho x\,dx$
  • Pumping liquid from a tank: $W = \int_a^b \rho g A(y) \cdot (\text{distance})\,dy$

Example 5.3

Problem: A 10-meter rope weighing 2 N/m hangs vertically from a ceiling. Find the work done in lifting the entire rope to the ceiling.

Figure — Work required to lift a hanging rope

A rope segment at distance $x$ from the ceiling must be lifted $x$ meters. Work is $W = \int_0^{10} 2x\,dx = 100$ J.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Model the force

A small segment of rope of length $dx$ at distance $x$ from the ceiling weighs $2\,dx$ newtons. To lift this segment to the ceiling requires work:

$$dW = 2x\,dx$$

Step 2: Set up the integral

The rope extends from $x = 0$ to $x = 10$ meters:

$$W = \int_0^{10} 2x\,dx$$

Step 3: Evaluate

$$W = 2 \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^{10} = \left[x^2\right]_0^{10} = 100$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{W = 100 \text{ Joules}}$$

Example 5.4

Problem: A cylindrical tank of radius 3 m and height 5 m is full of water. Find the work required to pump all the water over the top edge. (Water density: $1000\text{ kg/m}^3$, $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$)

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Set up a typical slice

Consider a horizontal slice of water at height $y$ (measured from the bottom) with thickness $dy$. The volume of the slice is:

$$dV = \pi r^2\,dy = 9\pi\,dy$$

Step 2: Compute the weight of the slice

The weight density of water is $\delta = 1000 \cdot 9.8 = 9800\text{ N/m}^3$. The weight of the slice is:

$$dF = 9800 \cdot 9\pi\,dy = 88200\pi\,dy$$

Step 3: Determine the distance to pump

The slice at height $y$ must be lifted a distance of $5 - y$ meters to clear the top of the tank.

Step 4: Set up the work integral

$$W = \int_0^5 88200\pi (5-y)\,dy$$

Step 5: Evaluate

$$W = 88200\pi \left[5y - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]_0^5$$ $$W = 88200\pi \left(25 - \frac{25}{2}\right)$$ $$W = 88200\pi \cdot \frac{25}{2}$$ $$W = 88200\pi \cdot 12.5$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{W = 1{,}102{,}500\pi \text{ Joules} \approx 3.46 \times 10^6 \text{ J}}$$

Center of Mass

The center of mass (centroid) of a planar region is the point $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ where the region would balance perfectly.

Centroid Formulas

For a region bounded by $y = f(x)$ (top), $y = g(x)$ (bottom), $x = a$, and $x = b$:

  • Area: $A = \int_a^b [f(x) - g(x)]\,dx$
  • x-coordinate: $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{A}\int_a^b x[f(x) - g(x)]\,dx$
  • y-coordinate: $\bar{y} = \frac{1}{A}\int_a^b \frac{1}{2}[f(x)^2 - g(x)^2]\,dx$

Example 5.5

Problem: Find the centroid of the region bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y = 4$.

Figure — Centroid of region bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y = 4$

The centroid $(\bar{x}, \bar{y}) = (0, 12/5)$ lies on the axis of symmetry for this region.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Find the intersection points

Set $x^2 = 4$ to get $x = \pm 2$. The region extends from $x = -2$ to $x = 2$.

Step 2: Compute the area

Using symmetry, integrate from $0$ to $2$ and double:

$$A = 2\int_0^2 (4 - x^2)\,dx$$ $$A = 2\left[4x - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2$$ $$A = 2\left(8 - \frac{8}{3}\right) = 2 \cdot \frac{16}{3} = \frac{32}{3}$$

Step 3: Compute $\bar{x}$ by symmetry

Since the region is symmetric about the y-axis:

$$\bar{x} = 0$$

Step 4: Compute $\bar{y}$

By symmetry, integrate from $0$ to $2$ and double:

$$\bar{y} = \frac{1}{A} \cdot 2\int_0^2 \frac{1}{2}\left[4^2 - (x^2)^2\right]\,dx$$ $$\bar{y} = \frac{3}{32} \int_0^2 (16 - x^4)\,dx$$ $$\bar{y} = \frac{3}{32} \left[16x - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^2$$ $$\bar{y} = \frac{3}{32} \left(32 - \frac{32}{5}\right)$$ $$\bar{y} = \frac{3}{32} \cdot 32 \cdot \frac{4}{5} = \frac{12}{5}$$

Final Answer:

$$\boxed{(\bar{x}, \bar{y}) = \left(0, \frac{12}{5}\right) = (0, 2.4)}$$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Surface Area

Find the surface area generated by rotating $y = x^3$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$ about the x-axis.

Show Answer

Answer:

$$S = \frac{\pi}{27}\left(145^{3/2} - 1\right) = \frac{\pi}{27}\left(145\sqrt{145} - 1\right)$$

Problem 2 — Spring Work

A spring with spring constant $k = 50$ N/m is stretched 0.2 m from its natural length. Find the work done.

Show Answer

Answer:

$$W = \int_0^{0.2} 50x\,dx = \left[25x^2\right]_0^{0.2} = 25(0.04) = 1 \text{ J}$$

Problem 3 — Center of Mass

Find the centroid of the region bounded by $y = x$, $y = 0$, and $x = 2$.

Show Answer

Answer:

$$A = 2, \quad \bar{x} = \frac{4}{3}, \quad \bar{y} = \frac{2}{3}$$

Centroid: $\left(\frac{4}{3}, \frac{2}{3}\right)$

Study Tips

1. For surface area, always sketch the region and identify the axis of rotation before setting up the integral.

2. When computing work, draw a typical slice at position $x$ (or $y$) and determine both the force on that slice and the distance it must travel.

3. Exploit symmetry whenever possible for center of mass problems. If the region is symmetric about the y-axis, then $\bar{x} = 0$.
