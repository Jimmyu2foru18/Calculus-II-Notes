Parametric Equations and Polar Areas

Parametric equations describe curves using a parameter $t$. They are especially useful for curves that cannot be expressed as a single function $y = f(x)$.

Key Formulas

Parametric Equations: $x = x(t)$, $y = y(t)$

Arc Length of a Parametric Curve:

$$L = \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} \, dt$$

Area in Polar Coordinates:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} [r(\theta)]^2 \, d\theta$$

Arc Length in Polar Coordinates:

$$L = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2} \, d\theta$$

Tangent Slope for Parametric Curves:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$$

Example 18.1

Problem: Find the parametric equations for the parabola $y = x^2 + 3x - 4$.

Figure — Parametric curve: parabola $y = x^2 + 3x - 4$

Parametric equations: $x(t) = t$, $y(t) = t^2 + 3t - 4$ trace this parabola.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Choose a parameter

Let $x = t$.

Step 2: Express $y$ in terms of $t$

$$y = t^2 + 3t - 4$$

Final Answer:

$x(t) = t$, $y(t) = t^2 + 3t - 4$

Example 18.2

Problem: Find the arc length of the curve $x = t$, $y = t^2$ from $t = 0$ to $t = 1$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Use the arc length formula

$$L = \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} \, dt$$

Step 2: Compute derivatives

$\frac{dx}{dt} = 1$, $\frac{dy}{dt} = 2t$

Step 3: Set up the integral

$$L = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 + (2t)^2} \, dt = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 + 4t^2} \, dt$$

Step 4: Use trigonometric substitution

Let $2t = \tan(\theta)$, then $dt = \frac{1}{2}\sec^2(\theta) \, d\theta$

When $t = 0$, $\theta = 0$. When $t = 1$, $\theta = \arctan(2)$.

$$\sqrt{1 + 4t^2} = \sqrt{1 + \tan^2(\theta)} = \sec(\theta)$$

$$L = \int_{0}^{\arctan(2)} \sec(\theta) \cdot \frac{1}{2}\sec^2(\theta) \, d\theta$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{\arctan(2)} \sec^3(\theta) \, d\theta$$

This requires integration by parts, giving:

$$L = \frac{1}{2}\left[\sec(\theta)\tan(\theta) + \ln|\sec(\theta) + \tan(\theta)|\right]_{0}^{\arctan(2)}$$

Final Answer:

$$L = \frac{1}{2}\left[2\sqrt{5} + \ln(2 + \sqrt{5})\right]$$

Example 18.3

Problem: Find the area inside the cardioid $r = 1 + \cos(\theta)$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Set up the area integral

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} (1 + \cos(\theta))^2 \, d\theta$$

Step 2: Expand the integrand

$$(1 + \cos(\theta))^2 = 1 + 2\cos(\theta) + \cos^2(\theta)$$

$$= 1 + 2\cos(\theta) + \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}$$

$$= \frac{3}{2} + 2\cos(\theta) + \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)$$

Step 3: Integrate

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} \left[\frac{3}{2} + 2\cos(\theta) + \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)\right] d\theta$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \left[\frac{3\theta}{2} + 2\sin(\theta) + \frac{1}{4}\sin(2\theta)\right]_{0}^{2\pi}$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \left[(3\pi) + 0 + 0 - 0\right]$$

$$= \frac{3\pi}{2}$$

Final Answer:

The area is $\frac{3\pi}{2}$.

Example 18.4

Problem: Find the slope of the tangent line to the curve $x = t^2$, $y = t^3$ at $t = 2$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Compute derivatives

$$\frac{dx}{dt} = 2t, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = 3t^2$$

Step 2: Use the chain rule

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{3t^2}{2t} = \frac{3t}{2}$$

Step 3: Evaluate at $t = 2$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{t=2} = \frac{3(2)}{2} = 3$$

Final Answer:

The slope of the tangent line at $t = 2$ is 3.

Example 18.5

Problem: Find the arc length of the curve $r = \theta$ from $\theta = 0$ to $\theta = 2\pi$ (spiral of Archimedes).

Figure — Archimedean spiral $r = \theta$

The Archimedean spiral $r = \theta$ has arc length $L = \int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{\theta^2 + 1}\,d\theta$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Use the polar arc length formula

$$L = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \sqrt{r^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\theta}\right)^2} \, d\theta$$

Step 2: Compute $r$ and $\frac{dr}{d\theta}$

$r = \theta$, so $\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 1$

Step 3: Set up the integral

$$L = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \sqrt{\theta^2 + 1} \, d\theta$$

Use the formula $\int \sqrt{x^2 + a^2} \, dx = \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{x^2+a^2} + \frac{a^2}{2}\ln(x + \sqrt{x^2+a^2})$:

$$L = \left[\frac{\theta}{2}\sqrt{\theta^2+1} + \frac{1}{2}\ln(\theta + \sqrt{\theta^2+1})\right]_{0}^{2\pi}$$

$$= \pi\sqrt{4\pi^2+1} + \frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi + \sqrt{4\pi^2+1})$$

Final Answer:

$$L = \pi\sqrt{4\pi^2+1} + \frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi + \sqrt{4\pi^2+1})$$

Example 18.6

Problem: Find the area inside the circle $r = 2$ and outside the cardioid $r = 1 + \cos(\theta)$.

Figure — Area between polar curves

The shaded region between the circle $r=2$ and cardioid $r=1+\cos\theta$ has area $5\pi/2$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Find the points of intersection

$2 = 1 + \cos(\theta) \Rightarrow \cos(\theta) = 1 \Rightarrow \theta = 0$.

By symmetry, the curves also meet at $\theta = 2\pi$.

Step 2: Set up the area integral

The area between two polar curves is:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \left(r_{\text{outer}}^2 - r_{\text{inner}}^2\right) d\theta$$

Here $r_{\text{outer}} = 2$ and $r_{\text{inner}} = 1 + \cos(\theta)$.

Step 3: Evaluate

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} \left[4 - (1 + \cos(\theta))^2\right] d\theta$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} \left[3 - 2\cos(\theta) - \cos^2(\theta)\right] d\theta$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} \left[\frac{5}{2} - 2\cos(\theta) - \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)\right] d\theta$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \left[\frac{5\theta}{2} - 2\sin(\theta) - \frac{1}{4}\sin(2\theta)\right]_{0}^{2\pi}$$

$$= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 5\pi = \frac{5\pi}{2}$$

Final Answer:

The area is $\frac{5\pi}{2}$.

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Parametric Equations for a Circle

Find parametric equations for the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 9$.

Show Answer

Answer:

$x(t) = 3\cos(t)$, $y(t) = 3\sin(t)$ for $0 \leq t < 2\pi$.

Problem 2 — Arc Length of a Line Segment

Find the arc length of the line segment from $(1, 2)$ to $(4, 6)$ using parametric equations.

Show Answer

Answer:

Parametrize: $x(t) = 1 + 3t$, $y(t) = 2 + 4t$ for $0 \leq t \leq 1$.

$\frac{dx}{dt} = 3$, $\frac{dy}{dt} = 4$

$L = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{9 + 16} \, dt = \int_{0}^{1} 5 \, dt = 5$.

Problem 3 — Area of a Polar Region

Find the area of the region bounded by $r = 2(1 + \cos(\theta))$.

Show Answer

Answer:

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{2\pi} [2(1+\cos(\theta))]^2 \, d\theta = 2 \int_{0}^{2\pi} (1 + 2\cos(\theta) + \cos^2(\theta)) \, d\theta$$

$$= 2 \int_{0}^{2\pi} \left[\frac{3}{2} + 2\cos(\theta) + \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)\right] d\theta = 2 \cdot 3\pi = 6\pi$$

Problem 4 — Tangent Line to a Parametric Curve

Find the equation of the tangent line to the curve $x = t^2$, $y = t^3$ at $t = 1$.

Show Answer

Answer:

At $t = 1$: $x = 1$, $y = 1$. Slope $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{3t^2}{2t} = \frac{3}{2}$ at $t = 1$.

Equation: $y - 1 = \frac{3}{2}(x - 1)$, or $y = \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{1}{2}$.

Problem 5 — Area Inside One Loop of a Rose

Find the area of one loop of the four-leaved rose $r = 2\cos(2\theta)$.

Show Answer

Answer:

One loop is traced from $\theta = -\frac{\pi}{4}$ to $\theta = \frac{\pi}{4}$.

$$A = \frac{1}{2} \int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} (2\cos(2\theta))^2 \, d\theta = 2 \int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} \cos^2(2\theta) \, d\theta$$

$$= 2 \int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} \frac{1 + \cos(4\theta)}{2} \, d\theta = \left[\theta + \frac{1}{4}\sin(4\theta)\right]_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} = \frac{\pi}{2}$$

Problem 6 — Arc Length of a Polar Curve

Find the arc length of the circle $r = 3$ from $\theta = 0$ to $\theta = \pi$ using the polar arc length formula.

Show Answer

Answer:

$r = 3$, $\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 0$

$$L = \int_{0}^{\pi} \sqrt{9 + 0} \, d\theta = \int_{0}^{\pi} 3 \, d\theta = 3\pi$$

(This is half the circumference of a circle with radius 3.)

Study Tips

1. For parametric curves, always find $\frac{dx}{dt}$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}$ before setting up integrals.

2. Sketch polar regions before integrating to determine correct limits.

3. Use symmetry to simplify polar area calculations when possible.

4. The polar arc length formula requires both $r$ and $\frac{dr}{d\theta}$.

5. For tangent lines in parametric form, first find the point $(x(t), y(t))$ and the slope $\frac{dy}{dx}$.
