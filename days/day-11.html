Sequences

A sequence is a function whose domain is the set of positive integers. We are typically interested in the limit of a sequence as $n \to \infty$.

Sequences

A sequence $\{a_n\}$ converges to $L$ if $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = L$. If the limit does not exist, the sequence diverges.

Useful limit: $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n^p} = 0$ for any $p > 0$.

Squeeze Theorem: If $a_n \leq b_n \leq c_n$ and $\lim a_n = \lim c_n = L$, then $\lim b_n = L$.

Example 1.1

Problem: Find $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2n + 7}{3n + 5}$.

Figure — Sequence convergence to $2/3$

The sequence terms $a_n = (2n+7)/(3n+5)$ approach the limit $2/3$ as $n \to \infty$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Divide numerator and denominator by $n$:

$$\frac{2n + 7}{3n + 5} = \frac{2 + \frac{7}{n}}{3 + \frac{5}{n}}$$

Step 2:

Take the limit as $n \to \infty$:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2n + 7}{3n + 5} = \frac{2 + 0}{3 + 0} = \frac{2}{3}$$

Final Answer:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2n + 7}{3n + 5} = \frac{2}{3}$$

Example 1.2

Problem: Find $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\ln(n)}{n}$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

This is of the form $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. Apply L'Hopital's Rule (treating $n$ as a continuous variable for the limit):

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\ln(n)}{n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1/n}{1} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} = 0$$

Final Answer:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\ln(n)}{n} = 0$$

Series Introduction

An infinite series is the sum of the terms of a sequence. We write $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ and study whether the sum approaches a finite value.

Geometric Series

A geometric series has the form $\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} ar^n$.

It converges to $\displaystyle \frac{a}{1 - r}$ when $|r| < 1$, and diverges when $|r| \geq 1$.

Partial sums: $S_n = a + ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^n$.

Example 2.1

Problem: Determine whether $\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{3}{2^n}$ converges, and if so, find its sum.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Identify as a geometric series with $a = 3$ and $r = \frac{1}{2}$.

Step 2:

Since $|r| = \frac{1}{2} < 1$, the series converges.

Final Answer:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{3}{2^n} = \frac{3}{1 - \frac{1}{2}} = 6$$

L'Hopital's Rule

L'Hopital's Rule applies to limits of the form $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$. It states that if $\lim f(x) = \lim g(x) = 0$ or $\infty$, then:

$$\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to c} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$

provided the limit on the right exists.

Indeterminate Forms

Forms $\frac{0}{0}$ and $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$: Apply L'Hopital's Rule directly.

Form $0 \cdot \infty$: Rewrite as $\frac{0}{1/\infty}$ or $\frac{\infty}{1/0}$ to create a quotient.

Form $\infty - \infty$: Combine into a single fraction using a common denominator.

Forms $0^0$, $1^{\infty}$, $\infty^0$: Take the natural logarithm and use L'Hopital's Rule on the resulting product.

Example 3.1

Problem: Evaluate $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to 0^+} x\ln(x)$.

Figure — Indeterminate form $0 \cdot (-\infty)$

As $x \to 0^+$, $x\ln(x) \to 0$ despite $\ln(x) \to -\infty$. The limit is 0.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Identify the indeterminate form. As $x \to 0^+$, $\ln(x) \to -\infty$, so $x\ln(x)$ has the form $0 \cdot (-\infty)$.

Step 2:

Rewrite as a quotient:

$$x\ln(x) = \frac{\ln(x)}{1/x}$$

Step 3:

Apply L'Hopital's Rule to $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$:

$$\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\ln(x)}{1/x} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1/x}{-1/x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0^+} (-x) = 0$$

Final Answer:

$$\lim_{x \to 0^+} x\ln(x) = 0$$

Example 3.2

Problem: Evaluate $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{2}{x}\right)^x$.

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

This has the form $1^{\infty}$. Let $y = \left(1 + \frac{2}{x}\right)^x$ and take the natural logarithm:

$$\ln(y) = x\ln\left(1 + \frac{2}{x}\right)$$

Step 2:

Rewrite as a quotient to apply L'Hopital's Rule:

$$\ln(y) = \frac{\ln\left(1 + \frac{2}{x}\right)}{1/x}$$

As $x \to \infty$, this has the form $\frac{0}{0}$.

Step 3:

Apply L'Hopital's Rule. Let $u = 1 + \frac{2}{x}$, so $\frac{du}{dx} = -\frac{2}{x^2}$:

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\ln\left(1 + \frac{2}{x}\right)}{1/x} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{-\frac{2}{x^2}\left(1 + \frac{2}{x}\right)^{-1}}{-\frac{1}{x^2}} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2}{1 + \frac{2}{x}} = 2$$

Step 4:

Exponentiate to find $y$:

$$y = e^{\ln(y)} = e^2$$

Final Answer:

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{2}{x}\right)^x = e^2$$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Find $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^2 + 1}{3n^2 - 2}$

Evaluate the limit of the sequence.

Show Answer

Answer:

Divide by $n^2$:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^2 + 1}{3n^2 - 2} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1 + \frac{1}{n^2}}{3 - \frac{2}{n^2}} = \frac{1}{3}$$
Problem 2 — Determine whether $\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{5}{3^n}$ converges

Identify the series type and find its sum if it converges.

Show Answer

Answer:

This is geometric with $a = 5$ and $r = \frac{1}{3}$. Since $|r| < 1$, it converges to:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{5}{3^n} = \frac{5}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \frac{15}{2}$$
Problem 3 — Evaluate $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{3x}$

Use L'Hopital's Rule or the standard limit.

Show Answer

Answer:

Using L'Hopital's Rule:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{3x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5\cos(5x)}{3} = \frac{5}{3}$$

Alternatively, $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(kx)}{x} = k$, so the limit is $\frac{5}{3}$.

Problem 4 — Find $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\frac{n}{n + 1}\right)^n$

Use logarithms and L'Hopital's Rule.

Show Answer

Answer:

Let $y = \left(\frac{n}{n + 1}\right)^n$. Then $\ln(y) = n\ln\left(\frac{n}{n + 1}\right) = n\ln\left(1 - \frac{1}{n + 1}\right)$. As $n \to \infty$, this has the form $\infty \cdot 0$. Rewrite:

$$\ln(y) = \frac{\ln\left(1 - \frac{1}{n + 1}\right)}{1/n} \sim \frac{-\frac{1}{n + 1}}{1/n} = \frac{-n}{n + 1} \to -1$$

So $y \to e^{-1} = \frac{1}{e}$.

Problem 5 — Evaluate $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\ln(x)}{\sqrt{x}}$

Use L'Hopital's Rule.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply L'Hopital's Rule twice:

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\ln(x)}{\sqrt{x}} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1/x}{1/(2\sqrt{x})} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2\sqrt{x}}{x} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2}{\sqrt{x}} = 0$$
Problem 6 — Determine whether $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}$ converges

Identify as a $p$-series and state the result.

Show Answer

Answer:

This is a $p$-series with $p = 2 > 1$, so it converges. In fact, $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$.

Study Tips

1. For sequence limits, divide numerator and denominator by the highest power of $n$ when dealing with rational functions.

2. Geometric series are the easiest series to evaluate. Memorize the formula and the convergence condition $|r| < 1$.

3. L'Hopital's Rule can be applied repeatedly as long as the indeterminate form persists.

4. For $0 \cdot \infty$ forms, rewriting as a quotient is the key step. Try both $\frac{0}{1/\infty}$ and $\frac{\infty}{1/0}$ to see which gives a cleaner derivative.

5. For $1^{\infty}$ forms, always take the natural log first, evaluate the limit, then exponentiate at the end.
