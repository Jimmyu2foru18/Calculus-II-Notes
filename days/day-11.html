Problem 1: Improper Integral with p = 3/4

Task: Determine if integral from 0 to 1 of 1/(x^(3/4)) dx converges or diverges.

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the type

This is an improper integral of Type II (infinite discontinuity at x = 0).

Step 2: Set up as a limit

integral from 0 to 1 of x^(-3/4) dx = lim(a->0+) integral from a to 1 of x^(-3/4) dx

Step 3: Integrate

= lim(a->0+) [x^(1/4)/(1/4)] from a to 1

= lim(a->0+) [4x^(1/4)] from a to 1

Step 4: Evaluate the limit

= 4(1) - 4(0) = 4

Final Answer:

The integral converges to 4.

Problem 2: L'Hopital's Rule

Task: Evaluate lim(x->0+) x*ln(x)

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Identify the indeterminate form

As x -> 0+, ln(x) -> -infinity, so we have 0 * (-infinity).

Step 2: Rewrite as a quotient

x*ln(x) = ln(x)/(1/x)

Step 3: Apply L'Hopital's Rule

lim(x->0+) ln(x)/(1/x) = lim(x->0+) (1/x)/(-1/x^2) = lim(x->0+) (-x) = 0

Final Answer:

lim(x->0+) x*ln(x) = 0

Problem 3: Sequences and Limits

Task: Find the limit of the sequence a_n = (2n + 7)/(3n + 5)

Click to see the solution

Step 1: Divide numerator and denominator by n

a_n = (2 + 7/n)/(3 + 5/n)

Step 2: Take the limit as n -> infinity

lim(n->infinity) a_n = (2 + 0)/(3 + 0) = 2/3

Final Answer:

lim(n->infinity) (2n+7)/(3n+5) = 2/3
