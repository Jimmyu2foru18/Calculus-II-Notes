Ratio Test

The Ratio Test examines the limit of the ratio of consecutive terms. It is particularly effective for series involving factorials or exponential terms.

\[ \rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| \]
Ratio Test

Key insight: If $\rho < 1$, the series converges absolutely. If $\rho > 1$ (including $\infty$), the series diverges. If $\rho = 1$, the test is inconclusive.

Root Test

The Root Test examines the $n$-th root of the absolute value of the terms. It is most useful when every term is raised to the $n$-th power.

\[ \rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \sqrt[n]{|a_n|} \]
Root Test

Alternating Series Test

An alternating series $\sum (-1)^{n+1}a_n$ converges if three conditions are met: the terms are positive, decreasing, and approach zero.

\[ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{n+1}a_n \text{ converges if } a_n > 0,\ a_{n+1} \le a_n,\ \text{and } \lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = 0 \]
Alternating Series Test (Leibniz Criterion)

Warning: The Alternating Series Test only proves conditional convergence. Always check absolute convergence separately if required.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Ratio Test

Task: Test $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n!}{n^2}$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Compute the ratio of consecutive terms:

$$\rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{(n+1)!/(n+1)^2}{n!/n^2}$$

Step 2:

Simplify:

$$= \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{(n+1)n!}{(n+1)^2} \cdot \frac{n^2}{n!} = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{n^2}{n+1}$$

Final Answer:

$$= \infty$$ Since $\rho = \infty > 1$, the series diverges.

Example 2: Power Series Interval of Convergence

Task: Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Apply the Ratio Test:

$$\rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{|x|^{n+1}/(n+1)!}{|x|^n/n!}$$

Step 2:

Simplify:

$$= \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{|x|}{n+1} = 0$$

Final Answer:

Since $\rho = 0 < 1$ for all $x$, the series converges for all $x$.

Interval of Convergence: $(-\infty, \infty)$

Radius of Convergence: $R = \infty$

Example 3: Alternating Series Test

Task: Test $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Check the three conditions with $a_n = \frac{1}{n}$:

$a_n = \frac{1}{n} > 0$ (TRUE)

$a_n$ is decreasing (TRUE)

$\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = 0$ (TRUE)

Final Answer:

All conditions satisfied. The series converges by the Alternating Series Test.

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Ratio Test

Test $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{5^n}{n!}$ for convergence.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test: $\rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{5^{n+1}/(n+1)!}{5^n/n!} = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{5}{n+1} = 0$.

Since $\rho = 0 < 1$, the series converges.

Problem 2 — Interval of Convergence

Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n}$.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test: $\rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{|x|^{n+1}/(n+1)}{|x|^n/n} = |x|$.

Converges for $|x| < 1$. At $x = 1$, the harmonic series diverges. At $x = -1$, the alternating harmonic series converges.

Interval of Convergence: $[-1, 1)$.

Problem 3 — Alternating Series Test

Test $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}$ for convergence.

Show Answer

Answer:

$a_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} > 0$, $a_n$ is decreasing, and $\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = 0$.

The series converges by the Alternating Series Test.
