Trigonometric Substitution

Trigonometric substitution converts integrals involving radicals into trigonometric integrals that can be evaluated using standard techniques. The substitution is chosen based on the form of the radical.

\[ \sqrt{a^2-x^2} \implies x = a\sin(\theta) \quad \sqrt{a^2+x^2} \implies x = a\tan(\theta) \quad \sqrt{x^2-a^2} \implies x = a\sec(\theta) \]
Trig Substitution Patterns

Key insight: Always draw the reference triangle when doing trig substitution. It helps with back-substitution and simplifies the algebra.

Power-Reducing Identities

Power-reducing identities express powers of sine and cosine in terms of first powers, making them integrable. These are especially useful for integrals involving $\sin^2(x)$ or $\cos^2(x)$.

\[ \sin^2(x) = \frac{1-\cos(2x)}{2} \qquad \cos^2(x) = \frac{1+\cos(2x)}{2} \]

Standard Trig Integrals

Two important standard integrals that frequently appear after trig substitution are the integrals of tangent and secant.

\[ \int \tan(\theta)\,d\theta = \ln|\sec(\theta)| + C \qquad \int \sec(\theta)\,d\theta = \ln|\sec(\theta)+\tan(\theta)| + C \]
Standard Trig Integrals

Worked Examples

Example 1: Basic Trig Substitution

Evaluate: $\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Let $x = 2\sin(\theta)$, so $dx = 2\cos(\theta)\,d\theta$.

Then $\sqrt{4-x^2} = \sqrt{4-4\sin^2(\theta)} = 2\cos(\theta)$.

Step 2:

Substitute into the integral:

$$= \int \frac{2\cos(\theta)\,d\theta}{2\cos(\theta)}$$

Step 3:

Simplify and integrate:

$$= \int d\theta = \theta + C$$

Final Answer:

Back-substitute using $\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)$:

$$= \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) + C$$

Example 2: Complex Trig Substitution

Evaluate: $\int \frac{x^3\,dx}{\sqrt{4-x^2}}$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Let $x = 2\sin(\theta)$, so $dx = 2\cos(\theta)\,d\theta$.

Then $x^3 = 8\sin^3(\theta)$ and $\sqrt{4-x^2} = 2\cos(\theta)$.

Step 2:

Substitute into the integral:

$$= \int \frac{8\sin^3(\theta) \cdot 2\cos(\theta)\,d\theta}{2\cos(\theta)}$$

$$= 8\int \sin^3(\theta)\,d\theta$$

Step 3:

Use $\sin^2(\theta) = 1-\cos^2(\theta)$ and let $u = \cos(\theta)$:

$$= 8\int (1-u^2)(-du) = 8\left(\frac{u^3}{3} - u\right) + C$$

Final Answer:

Back-substitute $\cos(\theta) = \frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2}$:

$$= \frac{(4-x^2)^{3/2}}{3} - 4\sqrt{4-x^2} + C$$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Basic Trig Substitution

Evaluate $\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{9+4x^2}}$

Show Answer

Answer:

Let $2x = 3\tan(\theta)$, so $dx = \frac{3}{2}\sec^2(\theta)\,d\theta$.

$$\sqrt{9+4x^2} = 3\sec(\theta)$$

$$= \frac{1}{3}\ln|\sec(\theta)+\tan(\theta)| + C = \frac{1}{3}\ln\left|\frac{\sqrt{9+4x^2}+2x}{3}\right| + C$$

Problem 2 — Power-Reducing Identity

Evaluate $\int \sin^3(3x)\,dx$

Show Answer

Answer:

Use $\sin^3(3x) = \sin(3x)(1-\cos^2(3x))$ with $u = \cos(3x)$.

$$= -\frac{1}{3}\cos(3x) + \frac{1}{9}\cos^3(3x) + C$$

Problem 3 — Power-Reducing Integral

Evaluate $\int \cos^4(x)\sin^2(x)\,dx$

Show Answer

Answer:

Use power-reducing identities to convert to cosines of multiple angles, then integrate term by term.

$$= \frac{1}{16}\left(x - \frac{1}{4}\sin(4x) + \frac{1}{6}\sin(6x)\right) + C$$
