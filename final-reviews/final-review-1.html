Integration by Parts

Integration by parts is used to integrate products of functions. Choose $u$ to differentiate and $dv$ to integrate such that the resulting integral is simpler.

\[ \int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du \]
Integration by Parts Formula

Key insight: For $\int x^n e^x\,dx$, let $u = x^n$ and $dv = e^x\,dx$. Repeated application reduces the power of $x$ until the integral is elementary.

Partial Fractions

Decompose a rational function into simpler fractions that can be integrated individually. Requires the degree of the numerator to be less than the degree of the denominator.

\[ \frac{1}{(x-a)(x-b)} = \frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{B}{x-b} \]
Simple Partial Fraction Decomposition

Warning: Always perform polynomial long division first if the numerator degree is greater than or equal to the denominator degree.

Series Convergence

A series converges if the sequence of partial sums approaches a finite limit. Several tests help determine convergence without finding the exact sum.

Key insight: Always check the limit of the terms first. If $\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n \neq 0$, the series diverges by the Divergence Test.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Integration by Parts

Evaluate: $\int x\ln(x)\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Choose $u = \ln(x)$ and $dv = x\,dx$.

Then $du = \frac{1}{x}\,dx$ and $v = \frac{x^2}{2}$.

Step 2:

Apply the integration by parts formula:

$$= \frac{x^2}{2}\ln(x) - \int \frac{x^2}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\,dx$$

Step 3:

Simplify and integrate:

$$= \frac{x^2}{2}\ln(x) - \frac{1}{2}\int x\,dx$$

Final Answer:

$$= \frac{x^2}{2}\ln(x) - \frac{x^2}{4} + C$$

Example 2: Partial Fractions

Evaluate: $\int \frac{1}{x^2-1}\,dx$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Factor the denominator and decompose:

$$\frac{1}{x^2-1} = \frac{1}{(x-1)(x+1)} = \frac{1/2}{x-1} - \frac{1/2}{x+1}$$

Step 2:

Integrate each term separately:

$$= \frac{1}{2}\ln|x-1| - \frac{1}{2}\ln|x+1| + C$$

Final Answer:

$$= \frac{1}{2}\ln\left|\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right| + C$$

Example 3: Power Series

Task: Find the interval of convergence for $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} x^n$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Apply the Ratio Test:

$$\rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{|x|^{n+1}}{|x|^n} = |x|$$

Step 2:

The series converges when $|x| < 1$.

At $x = 1$: $\sum 1$ diverges.

At $x = -1$: $\sum (-1)^n$ diverges.

Final Answer:

Interval of Convergence: $(-1, 1)$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Integration by Parts

Evaluate $\int x^2 e^x\,dx$

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply integration by parts twice with $u = x^2$ and $u = x$.

$$= (x^2 - 2x + 2)e^x + C$$

Problem 2 — Partial Fractions

Evaluate $\int \frac{3x+5}{x^2-4}\,dx$

Show Answer

Answer:

Decompose: $\frac{3x+5}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \frac{1/4}{x-2} + \frac{11/4}{x+2}$.

$$= \frac{1}{4}\ln|x-2| + \frac{11}{4}\ln|x+2| + C$$

Problem 3 — Series Convergence

Test $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n!}{n^2}$ for convergence.

Show Answer

Answer:

Apply the Ratio Test: $\rho = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{(n+1)!/(n+1)^2}{n!/n^2} = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{n^2}{n+1} = \infty$.

The series diverges since $\rho > 1$.
