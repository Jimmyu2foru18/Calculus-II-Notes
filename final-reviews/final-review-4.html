Polar Coordinates

Polar coordinates represent points using a distance from the origin and an angle from the positive x-axis. Converting between polar and rectangular forms is essential for analyzing curves and areas.

\[ x = r\cos(\theta), \quad y = r\sin(\theta) \qquad r = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}, \quad \theta = \tan^{-1}(y/x) \]
Polar ↔ Rectangular Conversion

Key insight: When converting $r = f(\theta)$ to rectangular form, multiply both sides by $r$ when possible to create $r^2$, then substitute $x^2 + y^2$.

Area in Polar Coordinates

The area enclosed by a polar curve $r = f(\theta)$ from $\alpha$ to $\beta$ is found by integrating the area of infinitesimal sectors.

\[ A = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta} [r(\theta)]^2\,d\theta \]
Polar Area Formula

Warning: Determine the correct limits of integration by finding where the curve starts and ends, or where it intersects itself. For symmetric curves, compute one portion and multiply.

Parametric Arc Length

For a curve defined parametrically by $x(t)$ and $y(t)$, the arc length is the integral of the speed over the parameter interval.

\[ L = \int_a^b \sqrt{[x'(t)]^2 + [y'(t)]^2}\,dt \]
Parametric Arc Length

Key insight: For $x = t$, $y = t^2$, the arc length integral becomes $\int_a^b \sqrt{1+(2t)^2}\,dt$, which requires trig substitution.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Polar Coordinates

Task: Convert $r = 4\cos(\theta)$ to rectangular form

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Multiply both sides by $r$ to create $r^2$:

$r^2 = 4r\cos(\theta)$

Step 2:

Substitute $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$ and $r\cos(\theta) = x$:

$x^2 + y^2 = 4x$

Final Answer:

Complete the square: $(x-2)^2 + y^2 = 4$. This is a circle of radius 2 centered at $(2, 0)$.

Example 2: Area in Polar Coordinates

Task: Find the area enclosed by $r = 1 + \sin(\theta)$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Set up the area integral over $[0, 2\pi]$:

$$A = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} (1+\sin(\theta))^2\,d\theta$$

Step 2:

Expand the integrand:

$$(1+\sin(\theta))^2 = 1 + 2\sin(\theta) + \sin^2(\theta) = 1 + 2\sin(\theta) + \frac{1-\cos(2\theta)}{2}$$

$$= \frac{3}{2} + 2\sin(\theta) - \frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)$$

Step 3:

Integrate term by term:

$$A = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{3\theta}{2} - 2\cos(\theta) - \frac{\sin(2\theta)}{4}\right]_0^{2\pi}$$

Final Answer:

$$= \frac{1}{2}(3\pi) = \frac{3\pi}{2}$$

Example 3: Parametric Arc Length

Task: Find the arc length of $x = t$, $y = t^2$ from $t=0$ to $t=1$

Click to see the solution

Step 1:

Compute the derivatives: $\frac{dx}{dt} = 1$ and $\frac{dy}{dt} = 2t$.

Step 2:

Set up the arc length integral:

$$L = \int_0^1 \sqrt{1 + 4t^2}\,dt$$

Let $2t = \tan(\theta)$, so $dt = \frac{1}{2}\sec^2(\theta)\,d\theta$.

When $t=0$, $\theta=0$. When $t=1$, $\theta=\arctan(2)$.

Step 3:

Substitute and simplify:

$$L = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\arctan(2)} \sec^3(\theta)\,d\theta$$

$$= \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{2}\sec(\theta)\tan(\theta) + \frac{1}{2}\ln|\sec(\theta)+\tan(\theta)|\right]_0^{\arctan(2)}$$

Final Answer:

$$= \frac{1}{4}\left[2\sqrt{5} + \ln(2+\sqrt{5})\right]$$

Practice Problems

Problem 1 — Polar Form Conversion

Convert $x^2 + y^2 = 6y$ to polar form.

Show Answer

Answer:

Substitute $r^2 = x^2 + y^2$ and $y = r\sin(\theta)$:

$r^2 = 6r\sin(\theta) \implies r = 6\sin(\theta)$.

Problem 2 — Polar Area

Find the area enclosed by $r = 2\sin(2\theta)$.

Show Answer

Answer:

The curve is a four-petaled rose. Integrate over one petal $[0, \pi/2]$ and multiply by 4:

$A = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi/2} 4\sin^2(2\theta)\,d\theta = 4\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^2(2\theta)\,d\theta = 2\pi$.

Problem 3 — Maclaurin Series

Find the Maclaurin series for $f(x) = e^{-x}$.

Show Answer

Answer:

Using $f^{(n)}(x) = (-1)^n e^{-x}$ and evaluating at $x=0$:

$$e^{-x} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^n}{n!} = 1 - x + \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{6} + \cdots$$

Interval of Convergence: $(-\infty, \infty)$.
